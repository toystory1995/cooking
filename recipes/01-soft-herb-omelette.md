The dish every French kitchen used to hire on. No colour, no browning, just custard
wrapped in a thin skin. If you can do this, you can control heat.

## Ingredients
- 3 eggs, at room temperature
- 15g butter
- 1 tbsp finely chopped soft herbs (chives, chervil, parsley, tarragon)
- Salt, white pepper

## Method
1. Beat the eggs with a fork for 30 seconds — until the yolk and white are one colour, no further. Season with salt now; it needs time to dissolve.
2. Melt the butter in the pan over **medium-low** heat until it foams but never browns.
3. Pour in the eggs. Wait five seconds, then stir constantly with the spatula in small circles, scraping the base, for about 45 seconds. You are making scrambled eggs on purpose.
4. When it looks like loose curds in a thin custard, stop stirring. Spread it flat and let it sit for 20 seconds to set the base.
5. Scatter the herbs. Tilt the pan away from you, fold the top third down with the spatula, then roll it out onto the plate so the seam lands underneath.
6. Rub the top with a little butter for shine. Eat within a minute.

## What you're learning
Heat management is the whole recipe. Eggs set at 62–70°C; a hot pan blows straight past
that and gives you rubber. Stirring keeps the curds small so the finished omelette is
creamy inside a set shell — the same principle behind custard, scrambled eggs and
carbonara.

## Push it further
Cook it three times this week and drop the heat a notch each time. Then try one with a
filling — grated Comté, or mushrooms cooked separately and drained — added just before
the fold, never in the raw egg.
