MasterChef's hardest rounds are tasting rounds: identify what is in this, or fix what is
wrong with it. Palate is trainable, and almost nobody trains it.

## Ingredients
- 500ml unsalted chicken or vegetable stock
- Salt, lemon, sugar, a bitter leaf, soy sauce, a piece of parmesan rind
- 8 or so single ingredients for blind tasting: herbs, spices, vinegars, oils, cheeses
- A helper to prepare things out of your sight

## The exercises
**1. The salt ladder.** Pour six small bowls of warm stock. Add salt at 0%, 0.25%, 0.5%, 0.75%, 1% and 1.5% by weight. Taste in order, cleansing with water. Somewhere around 0.8–1% the liquid stops tasting *salty* and starts tasting *of itself*. Find your threshold and write it down. This is the single most useful number in your cooking.

**2. The five tastes.** Make five glasses: salt, sugar, lemon juice, tonic water or bitter leaf infusion, and soy or parmesan broth for umami. Taste each and note **where** on your tongue and how long it lingers. Acidity arrives fast and leaves; bitterness arrives last and stays.

**3. Fault-finding.** Have your helper make four small bowls of the same soup: one correct, one under-salted, one over-acidified, one flat and over-sweet. Taste blind and name the fault. Then fix each one, and note what you used: salt for flat, acid for heavy, fat or sugar for harsh, more of everything for dull.

**4. Blind identification.** Eight small bowls, eyes closed or blindfolded. Name each ingredient, then describe it in three words that are not the name. This is the vocabulary you will need if you are ever asked to talk about your food.

**5. Temperature.** Taste the same soup hot, warm and cold. Salt and sweetness read very differently, which is why cold dishes need more seasoning than you think.

## What you're learning
Calibration of your own palate, and a repair vocabulary: when a dish is *nearly* right, you
want to know instantly whether it needs salt, acid, fat, sweetness or heat. Guessing costs
minutes you will not have.

## Push it further
Repeat exercise 1 every few months — your threshold drifts, usually upward. Then start
tasting everything you cook against the question "what does this need?" rather than "is
this nice?".
