One bird, eight portions, two meals and a stock. Buying jointed chicken costs roughly twice
as much and teaches you nothing about how the animal is put together.

## Ingredients
- 1 whole chicken, 1.5–1.8kg
- Nothing else. This is a technique, not a dish.

## Method
1. **Legs.** Pull a leg away from the body and cut through the skin between thigh and breast. Bend the leg back sharply until the hip joint pops out of its socket. Cut through that exposed joint — you are cutting cartilage, not bone. As you finish, curl the knife around the back to take the *oyster*, the coin of dark meat sitting in the hollow of the carcass. Repeat.
2. **Split the leg** into thigh and drumstick: turn it skin-side down and look for the thin line of fat running across the joint. Cut straight through it.
3. **Wings.** Feel for the shoulder joint, cut through it, and take the wing with the little nub of breast meat attached.
4. **Breasts.** Run your knife down one side of the breastbone, keeping the blade against the bone, and peel the meat away with short strokes, following the ribcage. Take it off in one piece. Repeat.
5. **Bone a thigh:** skin-side down, cut along the length of the bone, then scrape the meat away from each side and snip the bone free at both ends. Boned thighs cook flat and fast and are the most useful cut in the bird.
6. **Trim a supreme:** remove the tenderloin, pull out its white tendon by gripping it and scraping down the length with the knife, and square off the ragged edge.
7. **Carcass, wing tips and trimmings** go straight into the stockpot (recipe 06). Chicken skin trimmings, laid flat between two trays and baked at 180°C, become crackling for a salad.

## What you're learning
Joints, not bones. Every cut here goes through cartilage, and once you can feel a joint
through the skin, jointing rabbit, duck and lamb becomes the same job. You are also
learning yield — what the same money buys when you do the work.

## Push it further
Time yourself. A cook doing this daily takes about two minutes; get under five. Then do it
again and French-trim the wings into a mini rack.
