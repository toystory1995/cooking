Once you understand that the brown stuff stuck to the pan is dinner, half of restaurant
cooking opens up.

## Ingredients
- 4 bone-in, skin-on chicken thighs
- 1 tbsp neutral oil
- 1 shallot, finely diced
- 1 clove garlic, sliced
- 100ml dry white wine (or stock plus a squeeze of lemon)
- 150ml chicken stock
- 30g cold butter, cubed
- 1 sprig thyme
- Salt, pepper

## Method
1. Dry the skin **thoroughly** with kitchen paper and salt it 30 minutes ahead if you can. Wet skin steams; dry skin crisps.
2. Heat the oil in the skillet over medium-high until it shimmers. Lay the thighs in skin-side down, away from you. Press each one flat for a few seconds so the whole skin makes contact.
3. Leave them alone for 8–10 minutes. Do not move, poke or peek. The skin releases itself when it is ready.
4. Flip, drop the heat to medium and cook 6–8 minutes more, to 74°C at the bone. Move to a plate to rest.
5. Pour off all but a tablespoon of fat. Add the shallot, cook 2 minutes until soft, then the garlic for 30 seconds.
6. Pour in the wine and scrape every brown speck off the base with a wooden spoon — that is the *fond*, and it is where the flavour lives. Reduce until nearly dry.
7. Add stock and thyme, reduce by half. Off the heat, whisk in the cold butter a cube at a time until glossy. Taste, season, add resting juices from the plate.
8. Spoon the sauce **around** the chicken, never over the skin.

## What you're learning
The Maillard reaction — the browning that builds savoury flavour — plus deglazing and
*monter au beurre*, the cold-butter finish that gives a sauce shine and body without
flour. This single sequence is the skeleton of hundreds of dishes.

## Push it further
Swap the wine for cider and add a spoon of mustard; or vermouth, cream and tarragon.
Same method, different postcode.
