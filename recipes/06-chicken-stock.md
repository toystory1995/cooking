Not a recipe so much as a licence. Every good sauce in this library starts here, and
shop-bought cubes will hold your cooking back more than any missing gadget.

## Ingredients
- 1.5kg chicken bones — carcasses, wings, necks (raw or roasted)
- 2 onions, halved, skin on
- 2 carrots, 2 celery sticks, roughly chopped
- 1 leek top
- 10 black peppercorns, 2 bay leaves, few parsley stalks
- Cold water to cover by 5cm

## Method
1. If you want a brown stock, roast the bones at 220°C for 40 minutes until deeply coloured. For a pale stock, skip this and blanch the bones for 5 minutes, then rinse.
2. Put the bones in the pot and cover with **cold** water. Cold water draws proteins out slowly so they rise as scum instead of clouding the liquid.
3. Bring to a bare simmer — one lazy bubble every second or two. Never boil: boiling emulsifies fat into the stock and turns it greasy and cloudy.
4. Skim the grey foam off the surface for the first 20 minutes, patiently.
5. Add the vegetables and aromatics. Simmer uncovered for 3–4 hours, topping up if the bones surface.
6. Strain through a fine sieve without pressing the solids. Cool fast — sit the pot in a sink of iced water — then chill overnight and lift off the set fat.
7. To make a sauce base, reduce a litre down to 200ml. It should coat a spoon and taste concentrated but not salty. Never salt your stock; salt the dish it becomes.

## What you're learning
Extraction and clarity. Temperature control here is the same lesson as the omelette, on a
four-hour timescale. A stock reduced until it sets to jelly in the fridge is a *glace* —
a spoonful lifts almost any sauce.

## Push it further
Freeze it in ice-cube trays. Then make a second batch and reduce it by 90% to a glace, and
taste the two side by side to understand what reduction actually does.
