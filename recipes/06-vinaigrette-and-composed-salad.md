Judges taste the dressing, not the leaves. A salad is a seasoning exercise wearing a
disguise.

## Ingredients
- 1 tbsp Dijon mustard
- 1 tbsp red wine vinegar (or sherry vinegar)
- 90ml good olive oil
- 1 small shallot, minced
- Pinch of sugar, salt, black pepper
- Leaves: little gem, radicchio, herbs — whatever is crisp
- Optional: soft-boiled egg, toasted hazelnuts, shaved hard cheese

## Method
1. Steep the shallot in the vinegar with a pinch of salt for 10 minutes. This takes the raw bite out and seasons the acid.
2. Whisk in the mustard. Mustard is the emulsifier — it holds oil and vinegar together.
3. Add the oil in a slow, thin stream while whisking hard. It should thicken and go opaque. If it splits, whisk a fresh teaspoon of mustard in a clean bowl and dribble the split mix into it.
4. Taste on a leaf, not a spoon. Too sharp: more oil or a pinch of sugar. Flat: more salt before you reach for more vinegar.
5. Wash and dry the leaves properly — water is the enemy of dressing adhesion. Dress in a wide bowl with your hands, a little at a time, so every leaf is coated and nothing pools at the bottom.
6. Build the plate in layers rather than tossing everything into a heap: leaves, then nuts, then cheese, then egg halved and seasoned.

## What you're learning
Emulsification, and the four-way balance of fat, acid, salt and sweetness that you will
adjust in every sauce you ever make. The 3:1 oil-to-acid ratio is a starting point, not a
law — taste, then move.

## Push it further
Make three vinaigrettes side by side: sherry-Dijon, lemon-honey, and one with anchovy and
garlic pounded in. Taste them next to each other and write down which you would put with
fish, lamb and tomatoes.
