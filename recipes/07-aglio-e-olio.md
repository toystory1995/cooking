Five ingredients, nowhere to hide. Italians use this to judge whether you can actually
cook.

## Ingredients
- 200g spaghetti
- 6 cloves garlic, sliced paper-thin
- 80ml olive oil
- 1 dried red chilli, crumbled
- Large handful flat parsley, chopped
- Salt

## Method
1. Boil the pasta in water salted like the sea. Pull it 2 minutes before the packet says.
2. Meanwhile, put the garlic and oil in a **cold** pan and bring it up over low heat. Starting cold means the garlic toasts evenly instead of scorching in the middle.
3. When the garlic is pale gold — the shade of straw, not caramel — add the chilli and take the pan off the heat. Burnt garlic is bitter and unfixable.
4. Lift the pasta straight into the pan with tongs, along with a ladle of the starchy cooking water.
5. Back on medium heat, toss and swirl hard for a minute. The water and oil will come together into a glossy sauce that clings. Add more water in splashes if it looks oily or tight.
6. Off the heat, stir in the parsley. Taste for salt. Serve immediately in warm bowls.

## What you're learning
Starch emulsion: pasta water is not a rinse, it is an ingredient. The agitation of tossing
is what forces oil and starchy water into a sauce — the same physics behind cacio e pepe
and carbonara, which are the next two things you should cook.

## Push it further
Add a tin of good anchovies at step 3 and let them melt. Then try it with toasted
breadcrumbs on top instead of cheese, the southern Italian way.
