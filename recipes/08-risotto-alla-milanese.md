A classic MasterChef trap: cooked too far ahead it turns to porridge, rushed it stays
chalky. It must be made to order and eaten immediately.

## Ingredients
- 180g carnaroli or arborio rice
- 1 litre chicken stock, kept at a simmer
- 1 small onion, very finely diced
- 60ml dry white wine
- Good pinch saffron threads
- 40g cold butter, cubed
- 40g Parmesan, grated
- Olive oil, salt

## Method
1. Steep the saffron in a small ladle of hot stock. Keep the rest of the stock simmering beside you — cold stock stalls the cooking and makes the grains cook unevenly.
2. Sweat the onion in oil over low heat for 8 minutes until soft and completely colourless.
3. Add the rice and toast for 2 minutes, stirring, until the grains are hot and the edges look translucent with a white pearl at the centre. This *tostatura* keeps the grain intact.
4. Pour in the wine and stir until it has gone.
5. Now add stock one ladle at a time, stirring often, adding the next only when the previous has been absorbed. Keep it at a lively simmer, not a boil. 16–18 minutes.
6. Add the saffron stock at the halfway mark so the colour stays vivid.
7. Taste from minute 15. You want *all'onda* — it ripples like a wave when you shake the pan — and a grain with the faintest resistance at its core.
8. Off the heat, beat in the cold butter and Parmesan hard for 30 seconds. This is *mantecatura*, and it is what makes risotto creamy. Rest 1 minute, then plate: it should spread slowly on the plate, not stand up in a mound.

## What you're learning
Controlled starch release. The stirring rubs the grains together to free surface starch,
while the grain itself stays firm — creaminess without a drop of cream.

## Push it further
Make it again with mushroom stock and finish with thyme, then a green one with pea purée
stirred in at the end. Then try the Milanese with an osso buco on top, which is how it is
actually meant to be eaten.
