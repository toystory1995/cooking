Edge-to-edge pink with a hard crust. Ovens first, fire second — the opposite of what most
home cooks do.

## Ingredients
- 1 thick-cut sirloin or ribeye, at least 4cm, about 500g
- 1 tbsp neutral oil
- Flaky salt, coarse pepper
- For the butter: 100g soft butter, 1 anchovy mashed, 1 tsp Dijon, 1 small shallot minced, 1 tbsp chopped parsley and tarragon, ½ tsp capers chopped, squeeze of lemon

## Method
1. Mash the butter ingredients together, roll into a cylinder in baking paper and chill.
2. Salt the steak all over and leave it on a rack at room temperature for 45 minutes.
3. Oven to 120°C. Cook the steak on the rack until the centre reads **48°C** for medium-rare, roughly 25–35 minutes. Start probing early; thickness matters more than weight.
4. Rest it for 10 minutes off the heat. Because it cooked gently there is almost no internal pressure — this rest is about drying the surface.
5. Get the cast-iron pan properly, frighteningly hot. Add oil, then the steak. Sear 60–90 seconds a side, turning every 30 seconds for an even crust. Sear the fat cap by holding it upright with tongs.
6. The centre should now read 52–54°C. Rest 5 minutes, slice **across the grain**, salt the cut faces, and lay a coin of the butter on top so it melts into the slices.

## What you're learning
Why the sear happens last: a dry, already-warm surface browns almost instantly, so you get
crust without a grey band underneath. You are also learning to cook to temperature rather
than to time, which is the single biggest step up in meat cookery.

## Push it further
Do a side-by-side: one steak reverse-seared, one pan-seared from the fridge. Cut them both
and look at the cross-section. You will not go back.
