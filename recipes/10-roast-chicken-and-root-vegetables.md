The Sunday benchmark. Every cook should have one bird they can roast without thinking.

## Ingredients
- 1 free-range chicken, about 1.6kg
- 500g potatoes, carrots and onions, cut into large even chunks
- 1 lemon, halved
- Few sprigs thyme and rosemary
- 30g softened butter
- Olive oil, salt, pepper

## Method
1. Take the bird out of the fridge an hour ahead and dry it inside and out. Salt it generously all over, including inside the cavity.
2. Push the lemon halves and herbs into the cavity. Truss: a loop of twine around the legs, crossed, then pulled back along the body and tied. Trussing keeps the breast from drying out before the thighs are done.
3. Rub the butter over the breast and legs. Season again.
4. Toss the vegetables in oil and salt and spread them in the tin. Sit the chicken directly on top so the fat bastes them.
5. Roast at 220°C for 20 minutes, then drop to 180°C for another 50–60 minutes.
6. Probe the thickest part of the thigh, avoiding bone: **74°C** and you are done. Juices running clear is folklore; the thermometer is not.
7. Rest breast-side down on a warm plate for 20 minutes — non-negotiable. Meanwhile turn the oven up and give the vegetables a final blast.
8. Carve: legs off at the joint first, then breasts off the bone in one piece each, then slice across the grain. Pour the resting juices over.

## What you're learning
Carry-over cooking, and the fact that dark and white meat want different temperatures from
the same oven. Resting lets muscle fibres relax and reabsorb juice — cut early and it ends
up on the board instead of in the meat.

## Push it further
Next time, spatchcock it: cut out the backbone, flatten, roast at 220°C for 45 minutes.
Compare the two for evenness and skin. Keep the backbone and carcass — they are the next
recipe.
