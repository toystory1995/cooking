Cooking without heat. The whole dish is bought in the fishmonger's and finished in ten
minutes, which makes ingredient judgement the entire skill.

## Ingredients
- 300g very fresh firm white fish — sea bass, bream, halibut
- 8–10 limes (you want 150ml juice, squeezed to order)
- 1 red onion, sliced paper-thin
- 1 red chilli or half an aji amarillo, deseeded and sliced
- 1 clove garlic, 2cm ginger, small handful coriander
- Salt
- To serve: sweet potato, boiled and sliced; toasted corn

## Method
1. **Buy properly.** Sashimi-grade or straight off a trusted counter, and eaten the same day. Firm, glossy, no smell but clean sea. If it does not look like something you would eat raw, it is not ceviche fish.
2. Slice the onion and soak it in iced water for 10 minutes; this takes the harshness out and makes it crisp.
3. **Leche de tigre.** Blend a small offcut of the fish with the lime juice, garlic, ginger, a little chilli, coriander stalks, salt and 2 tablespoons of iced water. Strain. It should be cloudy, sharp, savoury, and taste finished on its own.
4. Cut the fish across the grain into even 2cm cubes. Even sizing is not cosmetic — the acid works from the outside in, so uneven pieces cure unevenly.
5. Salt the fish and let it sit 2 minutes. Then pour over the leche de tigre and stir gently.
6. **Wait 2–5 minutes, no more.** The surface turns opaque while the centre stays translucent. Beyond about ten minutes the acid keeps going and the texture turns chalky and dry — this is the same protein denaturation that heat causes, on a clock you can watch.
7. Fold in the drained onion, chilli and coriander leaves. Serve at once with sweet potato and corn, and drink the liquid left in the bowl.

## What you're learning
That acid denatures protein exactly as heat does, and that "raw" preparations are usually
the most technically exposed things you can serve. Also portion timing: this dish cannot be
plated in advance, which is a constraint worth practising.

## Push it further
Try the same method with scallops, and with cured then sliced fish (tiradito, cut in
sashimi slices and dressed at the last second). Then serve one plate of each and see which
your palate prefers.
