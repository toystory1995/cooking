Jarred paste will get you a meal. Pounding your own will teach you what a balanced dish
actually tastes like — and balance is what judges talk about most.

## Ingredients
**Paste:** 8 green bird's eye chillies · 2 shallots · 4 garlic cloves · 2 lemongrass stalks, tender part only · thumb of galangal · 1 tsp coriander seed, toasted · ½ tsp cumin seed, toasted · zest of 1 kaffir lime · handful coriander roots and stalks · 1 tsp shrimp paste · ½ tsp salt

**Curry:** 400ml coconut milk (do not shake the tin) · 400g chicken thigh, sliced · 2 tbsp fish sauce · 1 tbsp palm sugar · 4 kaffir lime leaves, torn · 150g Thai aubergines or green beans · Thai basil

## Method
1. Pound the paste in order of hardness: salt and dry spices first, then lemongrass and galangal, then chillies, then the soft aromatics, then the shrimp paste. Ten to fifteen minutes of real work until it is a smooth, fragrant paste. Pounding ruptures cells and releases oils; a blender chops and bruises instead.
2. Spoon the thick cream from the top of the coconut tin into a hot wok. Fry it, stirring, until it splits and you see clear oil pooling at the edges — this is *cracking* the cream, and it is the step most recipes skip.
3. Fry the paste in that oil for 3–4 minutes until deeply fragrant and darkened.
4. Add the chicken and turn it in the paste until sealed. Add the rest of the coconut milk, the lime leaves, fish sauce and sugar. Simmer 15 minutes.
5. Add the vegetables and cook until just tender.
6. **Now taste and adjust, in this order:** salty (fish sauce), sweet (palm sugar), sour (lime, off the heat). Keep tasting after each addition. The finished curry should hit all four notes and none should arrive first.
7. Off the heat, stir in Thai basil. Serve with jasmine rice.

## What you're learning
Layering aromatics, and the discipline of adjusting one variable at a time. That tasting
loop — salty, sweet, sour, heat — is the most transferable thing in this whole library.

## Push it further
Make a red paste next (dried chillies, soaked) and notice how differently dried and fresh
chillies behave. Freeze paste in tablespoon portions.
