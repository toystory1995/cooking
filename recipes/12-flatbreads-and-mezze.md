Bread cooked in three minutes on a dry pan, and the dips that go with it. Also your first
lesson in how much difference technique makes to something as plain as chickpeas.

## Ingredients
**Flatbread:** 400g strong flour · 250ml warm water · 7g instant yeast · 1 tsp sugar · 1 tsp salt · 2 tbsp olive oil

**Hummus:** 250g dried chickpeas soaked overnight (or 2 tins) · 1 tsp bicarbonate of soda · 150g good tahini · 2 cloves garlic · juice of 2 lemons · ice water · salt · cumin

**Alongside:** labneh (strained yoghurt), quick pickled onions, olives, tomato salad, olive oil

## Method
1. **Dough.** Mix everything, knead 8 minutes, prove 1 hour until doubled.
2. **Hummus.** Cook the drained chickpeas with the bicarbonate of soda in fresh water until they are **falling apart** — well past tender, 45–60 minutes. The bicarb raises the pH and breaks down the skins; underdone chickpeas are the reason home hummus is grainy.
3. Blend the hot chickpeas with garlic, lemon and salt. Then add the tahini and blend for a full **three minutes**, dribbling in ice water, until it goes noticeably paler and turns fluffy. Tahini emulsifies with water like a mustard vinaigrette, and this is the step that makes it silky rather than pasty.
4. Taste: it should be lemony and salty enough to sting slightly on its own, because bread mutes it.
5. **Bake the bread.** Divide into 8, roll to 3mm. Get a dry heavy pan smoking hot. Lay one in — it will blister in 45 seconds, then puff into a balloon as steam forces the layers apart. Flip, another 45 seconds, and straight into a cloth to keep soft.
6. **Build the table.** Hummus swirled in a wide bowl with a well of olive oil, labneh dusted with za'atar, pickles, salad, herbs. Spread things out — a mezze reads as generosity.

## What you're learning
That steam puffs bread, not yeast — the pocket comes from a violently hot surface. And that
"simple" dishes are exactly where technique shows, because there is nothing else on the
plate.

## Push it further
Bake half the batch on a hot stone at 250°C as pita, and use day-old ones for fattoush. Then
make tahini sauce, tarator and muhammara from the same jar of tahini.
