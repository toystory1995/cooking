Your introduction to fermentation, on a very forgiving canvas. Mostly waiting.

## Ingredients
- 500g strong bread flour
- 400g water (80% hydration)
- 10g fine salt
- 4g instant yeast
- 60ml olive oil, plus more for the tin
- Flaky salt, rosemary

## Method
1. Mix flour, water and yeast with your hand until no dry flour remains. Rest 30 minutes — this is the *autolyse*, where flour hydrates and gluten begins forming on its own.
2. Add the salt and squeeze it through the dough. It will feel slack and sticky. That is correct; resist adding flour.
3. Over the next two hours, do four sets of stretch-and-folds 30 minutes apart: wet your hand, pull one side of the dough up and fold it over, rotate the bowl, repeat four times. The dough gets visibly stronger and smoother each round.
4. Cover and refrigerate **overnight**, 12–18 hours. Cold fermentation builds flavour that a fast rise cannot.
5. Oil the tin generously. Tip the dough in, fold it into a rough parcel, and let it sit at room temperature for 2–3 hours until puffy and relaxed to the corners.
6. Oil your fingers and dimple all the way to the base, firmly. Drizzle with oil, scatter rosemary and flaky salt.
7. Bake at 240°C for 20–25 minutes until deep gold and hollow-sounding. Lift it out of the tin onto a rack immediately or the base steams.

## What you're learning
What time does to dough. Same ingredients, different schedule, completely different bread —
the clearest possible demonstration that fermentation is an ingredient.

## Push it further
Keep 100g of dough back, feed it flour and water and start a sourdough starter. Meanwhile
try the same dough pressed thin for pizza bianca.
