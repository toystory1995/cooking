Your first ferment, and the safest possible introduction: salt does the protecting, wild
bacteria do the work, and you taste it every day to decide when it is right.

## Ingredients
- 1 napa cabbage, about 1.2kg
- 80g coarse sea salt (no iodine, no anti-caking agents)
- **Paste:** 2 tbsp glutinous rice flour cooked to a paste with 200ml water · 60g gochugaru (Korean chilli flakes) · 6 cloves garlic · 20g ginger · 2 tbsp fish sauce · 1 tbsp salted shrimp (optional) · 1 tsp sugar
- 1 daikon, julienned · 4 spring onions, cut into batons

## Method
1. **Brine.** Quarter the cabbage lengthways through the core. Rub salt between every leaf, heaviest at the thick white ends. Leave 2–3 hours, turning every 30 minutes, until a thick stem bends without snapping.
2. Rinse three times in cold water and drain thoroughly, cut side down, for 30 minutes. Taste a leaf: pleasantly salty, like a good crisp. Too salty and the ferment stalls; too little and it spoils.
3. **Paste.** Cook the rice flour and water to a thin porridge and cool — this gives the bacteria a quick sugar to start on. Blend with the garlic, ginger, fish sauce, shrimp and sugar, then stir in the gochugaru.
4. Mix the paste with the daikon and spring onion. Wear gloves. Coat every leaf of the cabbage, working from the base out.
5. Pack tightly into jars, pressing down hard to eliminate air pockets, leaving 3cm of headroom. The brine should rise to cover the vegetables — anything above the liquid will mould.
6. **Ferment** at room temperature for 2–5 days. Loosen the lid daily to release gas and press the cabbage back under the brine. It will bubble and smell sour and lively.
7. **Taste every day.** When it is sour enough for you, move it to the fridge, where it will keep fermenting slowly for months and go on changing.

## What you're learning
Lacto-fermentation: salt selects for lactic acid bacteria, which acidify the brine until
nothing harmful can live there. Once you trust this, sauerkraut, pickles, hot sauce and
preserved lemons are all the same idea with different vegetables.

## Push it further
Use week-old kimchi fresh in salads, and month-old sour kimchi for jjigae and kimchi
pancakes. They are genuinely different ingredients, which is the point.
