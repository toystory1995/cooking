A braise is a lesson in patience and in building flavour in layers that you can still
taste separately at the end.

## Ingredients
- 1kg beef shin or chuck, in 5cm cubes
- 150g smoked bacon lardons
- 1 bottle full-bodied red wine
- 500ml brown chicken or beef stock
- 2 onions, 2 carrots, roughly chopped
- 3 garlic cloves, 2 tbsp tomato purée, 2 tbsp flour
- Bouquet garni: thyme, bay, parsley stalks, tied
- **Garnish:** 200g button mushrooms · 12 pearl onions · butter
- Neutral oil, salt, pepper

## Method
1. Optional but better: marinate the beef in the wine overnight, then drain and dry it thoroughly, keeping the wine.
2. Render the lardons in the casserole until crisp; lift them out.
3. Dry the beef and sear it in the fat in **small batches**, deep brown on every side. Crowding the pan drops the temperature and steams the meat — this step alone accounts for most of the finished flavour. Set the meat aside.
4. Sweat the onion and carrot in the same pot, scraping the base. Add garlic and tomato purée and cook 2 minutes until it darkens and loses its raw smell.
5. Return the meat, dust with flour, stir and cook 2 minutes to lose the raw starch.
6. Pour in the wine, bring to a boil and reduce by a third. Add stock to barely cover, the lardons and the bouquet garni.
7. Lid on, into a 150°C oven for **3 hours**. Check once an hour: it should never bubble hard, just tremble. Meat is ready when a spoon goes in with no resistance — that is collagen turned to gelatine, and it is a texture, not a clock.
8. Meanwhile glaze the pearl onions in butter and a splash of water until tender and shiny, and fry the mushrooms hard in butter until golden.
9. Lift the meat out. Strain the sauce, skim the fat, and reduce it until it coats a spoon. Return everything, add the garnish, season and rest at least 20 minutes.

## What you're learning
Collagen conversion, and why "fork tender" is the only real doneness test for tough cuts.
You are also learning that the braising liquid is a sauce in progress: straining, skimming
and reducing at the end is what makes it restaurant food.

## Push it further
Make it a day ahead and taste it cold, then reheated. Then run the same method with lamb
shoulder and white wine, or with oxtail — same structure, different bones.
