Glass-crisp skin, translucent centre. Fish is where cooks get caught out, because the
window between perfect and dry is about ninety seconds wide.

## Ingredients
- 2 salmon fillets, skin on, 150g each
- 1 tbsp neutral oil
- 50g butter
- 1 tbsp capers, rinsed
- Juice of half a lemon
- Salt

## Method
1. Dry the skin obsessively. Salt it and leave it on paper for 10 minutes, then dry it again — the salt pulls out moisture you want gone.
2. Heat oil in the pan until it just shimmers. Lay the fillets skin-down and press each with the fish slice for 20 seconds so the skin does not curl away from the heat.
3. Cook on medium 5–6 minutes. Watch the side of the fillet: opaque colour climbing up from the base tells you exactly how far the heat has travelled. Take it off when it is two-thirds of the way up.
4. Flip, add the butter, and cook 60–90 seconds while spooning the foaming butter over the flesh. That is *arroser* — basting, which cooks the top gently without a second dry surface.
5. Lift the fish out skin-side up. Let the butter cook on to a nutty brown, add capers, hiss in the lemon juice off the heat.
6. Spoon the sauce around, never on the skin.

## What you're learning
Reading doneness from the *side* of the fillet instead of poking it, plus beurre noisette:
milk solids browning at around 150°C to give butter a hazelnut flavour. It goes on fish,
gnocchi, cauliflower and scallops.

## Push it further
Try the same technique on sea bass and on a thick cod loin, and notice how differently the
three fish behave. Cod wants lower and slower; bass is over in three minutes.
