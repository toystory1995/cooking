Pillows or bullets — there is very little in between, and the difference is how little you
work the dough.

## Ingredients
- 700g floury potatoes (Maris Piper, russet), similar sizes, unpeeled
- 1 egg yolk
- 120–160g "00" flour, plus more for the bench
- Salt, nutmeg
- To finish: 80g butter, handful of sage leaves, Parmesan

## Method
1. **Bake** the potatoes whole at 200°C for an hour until soft. Baking rather than boiling keeps water out of the dough, which is the single biggest factor in how much flour you need.
2. Halve them and scoop the flesh straight into a ricer while still too hot to hold comfortably. Rice it onto the bench in an even layer and let the steam escape for 5 minutes.
3. Scatter over salt, a grating of nutmeg and about two-thirds of the flour. Add the yolk.
4. Bring it together with a bench scraper, folding rather than kneading, adding flour only until it stops sticking to your hands. **Stop the moment it holds.** Every extra gram of flour and every extra fold costs you lightness.
5. Cut off a piece, roll into a 2cm rope, cut into 2cm pillows. Roll each down a gnocchi board or the back of a fork with your thumb to make ridges that hold sauce.
6. Cook a test one in salted simmering water first — if it disintegrates, work another spoonful of flour into the dough. They are done about 30 seconds after they float.
7. Brown the butter with the sage until the leaves crisp. Lift the gnocchi straight into the pan with a splash of cooking water and toss to glaze. Parmesan, pepper, plate.

## What you're learning
Restraint. Also a genuinely useful diagnostic habit: cooking one test piece before
committing the batch, which is exactly what you would do under time pressure.

## Push it further
Try a batch with roasted squash or ricotta instead (*gnudi*), where the moisture problem
is much harder. Freeze raw gnocchi on a tray, then bag them, and cook from frozen.
