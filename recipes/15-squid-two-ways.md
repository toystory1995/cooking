Squid is either cooked in two minutes or in forty. Everything between is rubber, and
knowing which side you are on is a genuinely useful piece of cook's knowledge.

## Ingredients
- 2 whole squid, about 400g
- Olive oil, garlic, chilli, parsley, lemon
- For the slow version: 1 tin tomatoes, glass of white wine, onion

## Method
1. **Clean.** Pull the head and innards out of the tube in one steady movement. Cut the tentacles off just below the eyes and squeeze out the hard beak from the centre. Pull the transparent quill out of the tube and rinse the inside. Peel off the purple membrane — it comes away easily with a fingernail. Keep the wings.
2. Slit the tube open flat and lay it inside-face up.
3. **Score.** With the knife almost flat, cut a fine diamond pattern **two-thirds of the way through**, about 3mm apart. This does two things: it lets heat in fast, and it makes the flesh curl decoratively as it contracts. Cut the scored sheet into 4cm pieces.
4. **Fast.** Get a pan smoking hot with a film of oil. Dry the squid thoroughly, season, and cook in a single layer, untouched, for **60 seconds**, then toss for 30 more. Off the heat, add garlic, chilli, parsley and lemon in the residual heat. Onto the plate immediately.
5. **Slow.** Alternatively, leave the tubes whole and sliced into rings, and braise them with onion, wine and tomato at a bare simmer for **40–45 minutes** until a knife goes in with no resistance at all. Somewhere around 8 minutes they will seize up and turn tough; keep going, they come back.
6. Cook both in one session and taste them side by side.

## What you're learning
Collagen again, in a species where it behaves brutally: fast cooking beats it, slow cooking
dissolves it, and the middle ground is the failure. Once you have felt this with squid you
will understand octopus, cuttlefish and every cheap cut of meat.

## Push it further
Save the ink sac for a black risotto. Then try octopus, which is entirely the slow route,
and try the same score-and-sear on a cleaned cuttlefish.
