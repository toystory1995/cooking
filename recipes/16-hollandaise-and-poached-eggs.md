One of the five mother sauces, and a genuine test of nerve: too much heat and you have
sweet scrambled eggs.

## Ingredients
- **Reduction:** 3 tbsp white wine vinegar · 3 tbsp water · 1 shallot sliced · 6 peppercorns crushed
- 3 egg yolks
- 200g butter, melted and warm, milk solids left behind
- Lemon juice, cayenne, salt
- 4 very fresh eggs, splash of vinegar for the poaching water
- Toasted muffins or asparagus to serve

## Method
1. Simmer the reduction ingredients until about 2 tablespoons remain. Strain and cool slightly.
2. Whisk the yolks with the reduction in the bowl over a pan of barely simmering water — the bowl must not touch the water.
3. Whisk constantly for 3–4 minutes until the mixture triples in volume and reaches the *ribbon stage*: the whisk leaves a trail that holds for a second. It should be about 65°C. Lift the bowl off the heat every time it feels like it is getting away from you.
4. Off the heat now, dribble in the warm butter in a thin stream, whisking without stopping. Fast pouring is the usual cause of splitting; if it does split, whisk a teaspoon of hot water — or a fresh yolk — in a clean bowl and add the broken sauce to it drop by drop.
5. Season with lemon, salt and a whisper of cayenne. Hold it lukewarm, covered. It will not survive reheating.
6. **Poach:** bring water to 80°C with a splash of vinegar. Crack each egg into a sieve first to drain the loose white — this is the trick that gives you neat eggs without swirling the pan. Slide in, cook 3 minutes, lift onto a cloth.
7. Build: muffin, egg, sauce over. Season the top.

## What you're learning
The other kind of emulsion: lecithin from the yolk doing the work that mustard did in your
vinaigrette, held in a temperature window of roughly 60–70°C. Above it, the yolk protein
sets and the sauce breaks.

## Push it further
Turn leftover hollandaise into béarnaise (tarragon and shallot reduction) and serve it with
the reverse-seared steak. Two sauces, one technique.
