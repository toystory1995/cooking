Two days, three stages, one perfect square. This is your first proper exercise in *mise en
place* — doing the work ahead so service is calm.

## Ingredients
- 1.2kg pork belly, boneless, skin removed and reserved
- 1 litre chicken stock
- 200ml cider
- 2 star anise, 1 cinnamon stick, 6 peppercorns
- 2 onions, 2 carrots, 1 head garlic halved
- 3 tbsp honey, 2 tbsp soy, 1 tbsp cider vinegar
- Salt

## Method
**Day one.** 1. Salt the belly all over and leave for an hour, then rinse and dry.
2. Sit it on the vegetables in the tray, add stock, cider and spices so the liquid comes two-thirds up. Cover tightly with foil.
3. Braise at 140°C for 4 hours, until a skewer meets no resistance.
4. Lift the belly out onto a tray lined with paper, lay paper on top, put a second tray on it and weight it with tins. **Press in the fridge overnight** — this compacts the layers so it slices cleanly instead of falling apart.
5. Strain the braising liquid, chill it, lift the fat off.

**Day two.** 6. Cut the cold belly into neat 6cm squares with a long knife in one confident stroke. Square off the edges; the offcuts are the cook's lunch.
7. Reduce 400ml of the braising liquid with the honey, soy and vinegar to a glaze that coats a spoon.
8. Sear the squares fat-side down in a dry hot pan for 3–4 minutes until crisp and dark, then turn once. Finish in a 200°C oven for 8 minutes to heat through, brushing with glaze in the last 2 minutes — any earlier and the sugar burns.
9. Serve on something sharp and smooth: apple purée, or celeriac.

## What you're learning
Cook–chill–finish, which is how professional kitchens serve slow food fast. Also the
lesson that glaze goes on late, because sugar burns long before meat is hot.

## Push it further
Deep-fry the reserved skin into crackling for texture on the plate. Then apply the same
three-stage method to lamb breast or beef short rib.
