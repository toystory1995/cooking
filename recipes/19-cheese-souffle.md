A soufflé is 90% technique and 10% ingredients. It is also the classic MasterChef nerve
test, because it cannot be checked, held or fixed.

## Ingredients
- 40g butter, plus soft butter for the dishes
- 40g plain flour
- 300ml whole milk, warm
- 4 egg yolks
- 6 egg whites
- 120g strong cheese: Comté, Gruyère or a mature Cheddar, finely grated
- 1 tsp Dijon, grating of nutmeg, salt, cayenne
- Grated Parmesan and fine breadcrumbs for lining

## Method
1. **Prepare the dishes properly.** Brush soft butter up the inside in vertical strokes from base to rim, chill, butter again, then coat with Parmesan and breadcrumbs. The vertical strokes give the mixture a ladder to climb.
2. Make a thick béchamel: melt butter, stir in flour, cook 2 minutes without colour, then add the warm milk in three additions, whisking smooth each time. Cook it out for 5 minutes — it should be very thick, like wallpaper paste.
3. Off the heat, beat in the yolks one at a time, then the cheese, mustard, nutmeg, salt and cayenne. **Overseason it**: the whites will dilute everything you just tasted.
4. Whip the whites with a pinch of salt to **soft peaks that just flop at the tip**. Stiff, dry whites cannot stretch and will tear as the soufflé rises.
5. Beat a quarter of the whites into the base to slacken it. Then fold the rest in in two additions with a large spoon: cut down the middle, sweep along the bottom, lift over, quarter-turn the bowl. Stop while you can still see a few streaks.
6. Fill to the rim, level the top, and run your thumb around the inside edge to make a groove — this stops the mixture catching on the lip.
7. Bake at 190°C on a preheated tray, **without opening the door**, for 25–30 minutes until risen, browned and with the faintest wobble. Serve in the next 90 seconds.

## What you're learning
Foam stability. Air trapped in protein, expanded by oven heat, then set by that same heat
before it collapses. Every fold you make costs you volume, so folding well is a physical
skill worth practising on its own.

## Push it further
Once it works, make it *twice-baked*: turn the cooled soufflés out, sit them in a dish,
pour over cream and bake again. Restaurants do this because it removes the timing risk —
useful knowledge for a pressure test.
