Flour, egg, time. A skill you will use for the ravioli and the plated courses further up
this library.

## Ingredients
- 300g "00" flour, plus more for dusting
- 3 whole eggs + 1 yolk, at room temperature
- Pinch of salt
- Semolina for dusting the cut pasta

## Method
1. Mound the flour on the bench, make a wide well, crack the eggs into it with the salt.
2. Beat the eggs with a fork, drawing flour in from the inner wall a little at a time until it becomes a shaggy paste. Scrape the bench clean and bring it together.
3. Knead for a full **10 minutes**. It starts ugly and tight and turns smooth, elastic and slightly tacky. This is gluten developing; there is no shortcut.
4. Wrap and rest at room temperature for 30 minutes — resting relaxes the gluten so the dough rolls without springing back.
5. Cut into four. Flatten one piece and roll it through the machine on the widest setting. Fold it in three like a letter and roll again on the widest setting, three times. This laminates the sheet and squares it up.
6. Now go down one notch at a time, once per setting, to the second-thinnest. You should be able to see the shadow of your hand through it.
7. Dust with semolina, fold loosely into a flat roll and cut 6mm ribbons. Shake them out at once into loose nests.
8. Cook in heavily salted boiling water for **90 seconds to 2 minutes**. Finish in the pan with your sauce and a splash of pasta water, as in the aglio e olio.

## What you're learning
Hydration and gluten. Egg pasta is a low-hydration dough, which is why it takes hard
kneading and long resting. You will feel the moment the dough changes under your hands —
that feel transfers to bread, and to the croissants at level 5.

## Push it further
Roll the last piece to the thinnest setting and make pappardelle; and use offcuts to try
*maltagliati* in broth. Then colour a batch with spinach purée and learn how added
moisture changes the flour balance.
