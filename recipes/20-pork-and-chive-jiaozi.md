The dumpling everything else is built on. Make these until the pleat is automatic, because
har gow and xiao long bao are the same hands doing harder work.

## Ingredients
**Wrappers:** 300g plain flour · 160ml just-boiled water · pinch salt

**Filling:** 400g pork mince, 20–25% fat · 150g Chinese chives (or spring onion), finely sliced · 1 tbsp ginger, grated · 2 tbsp light soy · 1 tbsp Shaoxing wine · 1 tsp sugar · 1 tsp salt · 2 tsp sesame oil · 80ml cold chicken stock

**Dipping:** black vinegar, soy, chilli oil, slivered ginger

## Method
1. **Dough.** Stir the boiling water into the flour with chopsticks, then knead 8 minutes to a smooth, firm dough. Boiling water partly gelatinises the starch, which makes the dough stretchy and forgiving rather than springy. Rest, covered, 30 minutes.
2. **Filling.** Mix pork with everything except the chives and stock. Now beat the stock in **a splash at a time in one direction only** until it is all absorbed and the mixture is sticky and slightly whipped. This is what makes the filling juicy instead of dense. Fold in the chives last. Chill 20 minutes.
3. Fry a teaspoon of filling in a pan and taste it. Adjust salt now, not after you have wrapped fifty.
4. **Wrappers.** Roll the dough into a 2cm rope, cut 12g pieces, press flat. Roll each with the pin held still and the dough turning under your other hand: press from the edge to the centre and rotate, so you get a 8cm circle **thin at the rim, thicker in the middle**. That thick centre carries the filling; a uniformly thin wrapper bursts.
5. **Pleat.** A teaspoon of filling in the centre. Fold in half and pinch the top closed. Working from one side, push a small fold of the near-side wrapper against the flat far side and press. Five or six pleats a side. The finished dumpling should sit up on its base like a little boat.
6. **Potstickers.** Heat oil in the pan, sit the dumplings in flat-side down, fry 2 minutes until golden. Pour in 100ml water, lid on, steam 7 minutes. Lift the lid and let the water cook away until you hear the frying change pitch and the bases are crisp again.
7. Turn out onto a plate crisp-side up.

## What you're learning
Hot-water dough, and the beating technique that emulsifies stock into mince — the same
principle as a sausage farce. Also the potsticker sequence of fry, steam, fry, which is
three cooking methods in one pan.

## Push it further
Freeze them raw on a tray, then bag. Cook from frozen with an extra 2 minutes of steaming.
Then make one batch boiled and one steamed and decide which wrapper thickness suits each.
