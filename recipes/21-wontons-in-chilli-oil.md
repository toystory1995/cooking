Sichuan *hong you chao shou*. The dumpling is simple; the sauce is the lesson.

## Ingredients
**Chilli oil:** 40g coarse Sichuan chilli flakes · 1 tsp Sichuan peppercorns · 250ml neutral oil · 2 star anise · 1 cinnamon stick · 3 slices ginger · 2 spring onions · 1 tsp sesame seeds · ½ tsp salt · ½ tsp sugar

**Wontons:** 250g pork mince · 100g raw prawns, chopped · 1 tbsp ginger · 1 tbsp soy · 1 tsp sesame oil · 1 tsp sugar · white pepper · 40 shop-bought wonton wrappers

**Sauce base per bowl:** 1 tbsp black vinegar · 1 tbsp light soy · 1 tsp sugar · 1 clove garlic grated · chilli oil to taste

## Method
1. **Chilli oil.** Put the chilli flakes, sesame seeds, salt and sugar in a heatproof bowl. Heat the oil with the whole spices, ginger and spring onion to 160°C, then let it cool to about **130°C** and strain it over the flakes. Too hot and the chilli scorches bitter; too cool and it never blooms. It should sizzle steadily, not violently. Rest overnight.
2. **Filling.** Mix everything and beat in one direction until sticky. Fry a scrap and taste.
3. **Fold.** A scant teaspoon in the centre of a wrapper. Wet two edges, fold into a triangle and press out the air. Bring the two long corners together across the front and pinch. That is it — wontons want speed, not decoration. Keep the ones you have finished under a damp cloth.
4. Cook in plenty of gently boiling water for 3–4 minutes: they float, then need one more minute. A hard boil tears them.
5. Build the bowl **before** the wontons are ready: sauce base in the bottom, then the drained wontons on top, then a spoonful of chilli oil and its sediment, then spring onion. Toss at the table.

## What you're learning
Blooming: aromatic compounds in chilli and spice are fat-soluble, so hot oil extracts
colour and flavour that water never will. Temperature control decides whether you get
fragrance or bitterness — the same call you made with the garlic in aglio e olio.

## Push it further
Use the same oil on cold noodles, on cucumber salad and over fried eggs. Then try a
Cantonese wonton noodle soup where the same parcel goes into a clear broth instead.
