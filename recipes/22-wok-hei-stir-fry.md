*Wok hei* — "the breath of the wok" — is a real, physical thing: fat and food searing on
metal above 200°C, and it is why restaurant stir-fries taste of something your pan at home
does not.

## Ingredients
- 300g beef sirloin or rump, sliced thin **across the grain**
- **Velveting marinade:** 1 tbsp light soy · 1 tsp Shaoxing · 1 tsp cornflour · ½ tsp bicarbonate of soda · 1 tbsp water · 1 tsp oil
- 300g gai lan or tenderstem broccoli, blanched 60 seconds
- 3 cloves garlic sliced, 2cm ginger, 2 spring onions
- **Sauce, mixed in a bowl beforehand:** 1 tbsp oyster sauce · 1 tbsp light soy · 1 tsp dark soy · 1 tsp sugar · 2 tbsp stock · ½ tsp cornflour · sesame oil
- 3 tbsp neutral oil with a high smoke point

## Method
1. **Velvet the beef.** Mix the marinade into the meat with your hand until every slice is coated and the liquid is absorbed, then leave 30 minutes. The bicarb raises the surface pH so the proteins stay tender; the cornflour holds moisture in. This is how the beef at your local Cantonese place is silky.
2. **Mise en place is everything.** Everything cut, the sauce mixed, plates out. Once the wok is hot you have ninety seconds and no hands free.
3. Heat the dry wok until it smokes and the surface shimmers with heat haze. Then add the oil and swirl it up the sides — *hot wok, cold oil*, which is what stops food sticking.
4. Add the beef in a **single layer** and leave it 20 seconds to sear before moving it. Toss briefly until just browned but not cooked through, then tip it out. Overcrowding drops the temperature and you will stew it.
5. Reheat, add a little oil, then the garlic, ginger and white spring onion. Ten seconds, no more.
6. Add the greens, toss, then the beef back in. Stir the sauce and pour it around the **hot sides of the wok**, not into the middle — it caramelises on the way down.
7. Toss hard until the sauce thickens and glazes everything, about 30 seconds. Onto the plate immediately.

## What you're learning
Heat as a limited resource: a domestic burner cannot recover from a full wok, so you cook in
batches and work fast. Velveting is a genuinely transferable trick for any lean meat.

## Push it further
Cook the same dish with and without velveting, side by side. Then try a dry-fry — Sichuan
green beans — where the point is to drive moisture out rather than to seal it in.
