Three days, twenty-seven layers of butter, and no way to fake it. The most demanding thing
in this library, and the one that will most change how you think about dough.

## Ingredients
- **Détrempe:** 500g strong flour · 10g salt · 55g sugar · 7g instant yeast · 140ml cold milk · 140ml cold water · 25g soft butter
- **Butter block:** 280g high-fat butter (82%+), cold
- Egg wash: 1 egg + splash of milk

## Method
**Day one.** 1. Mix the détrempe to a smooth dough — 5 minutes, no more; you want moderate gluten, because over-strong dough fights the roll. Chill overnight.
2. Beat the cold butter between two sheets of paper into a neat 18cm square. Chill.

**Day two.** 3. Roll the dough to 20x40cm. Sit the butter block on one half, fold the other over, seal the edges. **Dough and butter must be the same firmness** — around 15°C. Too cold and the butter shatters into shards; too warm and it merges into the dough. This one judgement is the whole craft.
4. Roll out to 20x60cm and fold in three (a letter fold). Wrap, chill 45 minutes.
5. Repeat twice more, always rolling in the same direction and turning the dough 90° between folds. Three letter folds gives 27 layers. Chill overnight after the last one.

**Day three.** 6. Roll to 4mm thick, about 25x60cm. Trim the edges square — sealed edges cannot rise. Cut triangles with a 10cm base.
7. Stretch each triangle gently to about 25cm long, then roll from base to tip, firmly but without squashing. Tip tucked underneath.
8. Proof at **24–26°C** for 2–3 hours. Not warmer: above 28°C the butter melts out and you will see it pooling on the tray. They are ready when visibly puffy and wobbling like jelly when the tray is nudged.
9. Egg wash carefully, avoiding the cut layers. Bake at 220°C for 8 minutes, then 190°C for 10–12 more, until very deeply browned. Pale croissants are underbaked croissants.
10. Cool completely. Cut one across: you want an open, irregular honeycomb, not a bready spiral.

## What you're learning
Lamination — alternating layers of dough and fat, where steam from the butter's water
content inflates each layer in the oven. Temperature is the entire game, and you will fail
this at least once. Everybody does.

## Push it further
Use the offcuts for pain au chocolat, and try one batch with a 4-3-3 fold count to see how
layer count changes the crumb. Photograph every cross-section; the honeycomb is your
report card.
