A braise that uses almost no added liquid: the vegetables and the conical lid do the work,
condensing steam and returning it. It is also the clearest lesson in sweet against savoury.

## Ingredients
- 1kg lamb shoulder, in large chunks
- 2 onions grated, 4 cloves garlic
- **Spice:** 2 tsp ginger · 1 tsp cinnamon · 1 tsp turmeric · 1 tsp cumin · ½ tsp saffron soaked in water · 1 tsp black pepper · 1 tbsp ras el hanout
- 1 preserved lemon, flesh discarded, rind sliced
- 100g green olives, 100g dried apricots
- Bunch coriander and parsley, tied
- 3 tbsp olive oil, salt, 1 tbsp honey

**Preserved lemons (start a month ahead):** unwaxed lemons, quartered but joined at the base, packed hard with coarse salt into a jar, pressed down under their own juice for 4 weeks

## Method
1. Marinate the lamb in the spices, grated onion, garlic and oil for a few hours.
2. Put everything except the olives, apricots, lemon and honey into the tagine with **only 150ml water**. The grated onion will release a surprising amount of liquid.
3. Lid on, over the lowest possible heat — use a diffuser — for **2½ to 3 hours**. It should barely murmur. Check twice; add a splash of water only if it looks like catching.
4. At 2 hours, add the apricots, olives and preserved lemon rind so they keep their identity rather than dissolving.
5. When the lamb pulls apart under a spoon, take the lid off and reduce the sauce until it clings and the oil separates slightly at the edges — that split is the traditional sign it is ready.
6. Balance it now: honey for depth, more preserved lemon for sharpness, salt. The finished dish should be savoury first with sweetness in the background, never jammy.
7. Rest 20 minutes and serve with bread or couscous steamed over the pot.

## What you're learning
Braising in a closed, almost dry environment, and preserved lemon as a seasoning rather than
a garnish — fermented salt and acid together, which is a different thing entirely from
fresh lemon.

## Push it further
Make a chicken tagine with olives and a fish chermoula version, then steam couscous properly
by hand — three times, rubbing through oil and water between steamings.
