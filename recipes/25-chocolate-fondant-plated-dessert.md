Dessert is where MasterChef contestants most often come unstuck, because pastry is
chemistry with a stopwatch. Three components, each with its own failure mode.

## Ingredients
- **Fondant:** 120g dark chocolate (65–70%) · 120g butter · 2 eggs + 2 yolks · 100g caster sugar · 60g plain flour · pinch salt · cocoa and soft butter for the moulds
- **Tuile:** 50g icing sugar · 50g plain flour · 50g egg white · 40g melted butter · pinch salt
- **To serve:** good vanilla or salted caramel ice cream, crème fraîche, raspberries

## Method
1. **Moulds first.** Brush the darioles with soft butter in upward strokes, chill, butter again, then dust with cocoa and knock out the excess. Miss a patch and the fondant will stick and tear on turning out.
2. **Tuiles.** Whisk the tuile ingredients smooth and rest 30 minutes. Spread thinly through a stencil onto a silicone mat and bake at 170°C for 6–8 minutes until the edges colour. Drape over a rolling pin while hot to curve them; they set in seconds and are brittle once cool. Make double — you will break some.
3. **Fondant batter.** Melt chocolate and butter together over barely simmering water. Whisk eggs, yolks and sugar to the ribbon stage — pale, tripled, holding a trail. Fold the warm chocolate in, then sift over the flour and salt and fold just until combined.
4. Fill the moulds three-quarters full. **Chill at least 1 hour** — this both firms the batter and makes the timing repeatable.
5. **Bake a test one.** 200°C, 11–12 minutes from chilled. The top should be set and domed with the faintest wobble at the centre; the sides should just pull away from the mould. Turn it out and cut it open. Adjust by 30 seconds either way, then commit to that number for the rest.
6. Rest 60 seconds, invert onto the plate, lift the mould after 10 seconds.
7. **Quenelle** the ice cream: dip a spoon in hot water, drag it across the surface of the tub while rolling the wrist, and turn out a smooth three-sided oval. Sit it on a small pile of crumbs so it does not slide.
8. Plate: fondant off-centre, quenelle beside it, tuile leaning, a few raspberries, one spot of crème fraîche. Serve immediately — the whole point is the flow when it is cut.

## What you're learning
Calibration. The test bake is the real lesson: you are not following a time, you are
finding *your* time for *your* oven and moulds, then repeating it exactly. That is what
consistency means in a professional kitchen.

## Push it further
Serve four at once, all cut open at the table. If all four flow, you can cook this under
pressure.
