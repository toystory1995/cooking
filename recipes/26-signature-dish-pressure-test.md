No ingredients list this week. You have cooked your way up the library; this is where you
find out what you can do with it.

## The brief
Cook one plate that you would be happy to put in front of a judge. It must include:

- **A protein or centrepiece** cooked to a temperature you chose deliberately and can defend.
- **A sauce you made from scratch** — an emulsion, a jus, a gastrique or a purée. Not a shop-bought anything.
- **A textural contrast**: something crisp, crunchy or brittle against something soft.
- **An acid** somewhere on the plate that stops it being heavy.
- **Three components maximum.** Restraint is a skill; a crowded plate reads as panic.

## The rules
1. Write your timeline **before you shop**, working backwards from the moment the plate leaves the pass. Note the temperature of every plate and pan you will need.
2. Do all your prep first, in containers, and clean down before you start cooking. A tidy bench is a legible mind.
3. Three hours, start to plate. Set a timer where you can see it.
4. No recipe in front of you while you cook. Notes on the timeline only.

## The debrief — the part that actually matters
When it is plated, photograph it, then sit down and answer these in writing:

1. What would a judge criticise first? Be honest; you already know.
2. Was it seasoned enough? Taste it again, cold, an hour later.
3. What was the weakest component, and would the plate be better without it?
4. What went wrong on the timeline, and what would you move?
5. What would you change if you cooked it again tomorrow?

Then cook it again next week with those changes. A signature dish is not invented, it is
iterated — and being able to say precisely why every element is on the plate is what
separates a good home cook from someone who belongs in that competition.

## What you're learning
Everything. Also the habit that makes the rest of it stick: cooking, then judging your own
food harder than anyone else would.
