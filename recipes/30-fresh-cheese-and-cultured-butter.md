Two dairy transformations you can do in a home kitchen with no equipment worth the name,
and both taste dramatically better than what you can buy.

## Ingredients
**Ricotta / paneer / labneh:** 2 litres whole milk · 200ml double cream (for ricotta) · 1½ tsp salt · juice of 2 lemons or 3 tbsp white vinegar · (for labneh: 500g full-fat yoghurt and salt only)

**Cultured butter:** 600ml double cream · 3 tbsp live yoghurt or buttermilk · salt

## Method
1. **Fresh cheese.** Heat the milk and cream slowly to **85–90°C**, stirring so it does not catch. Take it off the heat, stir in the acid once, gently, and leave it alone for 10 minutes. Curds will separate cleanly from greenish whey. Stirring after the acid goes in breaks the curd and gives you grainy cheese.
2. Ladle — do not pour — the curds into a muslin-lined sieve. Drain 20 minutes for soft ricotta, an hour for firm, or press under a weight for paneer that you can cube and fry.
3. Salt to taste. Keep the whey: it is excellent in bread, for cooking grains, or as a brine for chicken.
4. **Labneh** is even simpler: salt yoghurt, hang it in muslin overnight, and roll the result into balls under olive oil.
5. **Cultured butter, day one.** Stir the yoghurt into the cream and leave it covered at room temperature for **12–24 hours** until it smells tangy and has thickened. This culturing is the entire difference between good butter and remarkable butter.
6. Chill it, then whip in a mixer (or shake in a jar) — it goes through whipped cream, then grainy, then suddenly **breaks** into yellow butter grains and thin buttermilk. Drain, keeping the buttermilk for pancakes or soda bread.
7. **Wash the butter**: knead it in a bowl of iced water, changing the water three or four times until it runs clear. Any buttermilk left in it will turn rancid within days — this step is not optional.
8. Work in salt, shape, wrap. Use within two weeks.

## What you're learning
The two ways of separating dairy: acid coagulation for cheese, mechanical agitation for
butter. Also that culturing — the same lactic bacteria as your kimchi — is a flavour tool as
much as a preservation one.

## Push it further
Make paneer and fry it in a curry, and use the whey to cook rice. Then try a brown butter
made from your own cultured butter and taste it against the supermarket version.
