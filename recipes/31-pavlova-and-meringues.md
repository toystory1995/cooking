Egg white and sugar, in three different ratios and methods, giving three completely
different results. Meringue is one of the most useful things in a dessert repertoire.

## Ingredients
**Pavlova (French meringue):** 4 egg whites at room temperature · 200g caster sugar · 1 tsp cornflour · 1 tsp white wine vinegar · ½ tsp vanilla

**To serve:** 300ml double cream whipped to soft peaks · passion fruit, raspberries, mango

**Italian meringue (to practise):** 100g egg white · 200g sugar · 60ml water

## Method
1. **Clean bowl, no fat.** Wipe the bowl with lemon or vinegar. A trace of yolk or grease and the whites will never reach volume — this is the most common failure and it is unrecoverable.
2. Whisk the whites on medium to **soft peaks**, then add the sugar a tablespoon at a time with the mixer running. Slow addition lets the sugar dissolve; dumped in, it weeps syrup in the oven.
3. Whisk to stiff, glossy peaks, then keep going for another 5 minutes. Test by rubbing a little between your fingers: **completely smooth, no grittiness**. Any grain means undissolved sugar.
4. Fold in the cornflour, vinegar and vanilla. The acid stabilises the foam and the starch keeps the middle marshmallowy.
5. Pile onto paper in a 20cm circle, making a shallow dip in the centre and swooping the sides up.
6. Bake at **120°C for 90 minutes**, then turn the oven off and leave it inside, door shut, until completely cold — ideally overnight. This is drying, not baking. Cracks come from cooling too fast.
7. Top with cream and sharp fruit no more than an hour before serving.
8. **Italian meringue, for practice.** Boil the sugar and water to **118°C**. Meanwhile whisk the whites to soft peaks. Pour the syrup down the side of the bowl in a thin stream with the mixer running, then whisk until the bowl is cool. This one is cooked by the syrup, holds for hours, and is what you pipe onto lemon meringue tarts and torch. Swiss meringue — whites and sugar warmed together over a bain-marie to 60°C, then whipped — sits between the two.

## What you're learning
Protein foam stability, the same physics as the soufflé, with sugar as the stabiliser. Three
methods and their uses: French for baking and drying, Swiss for buttercream, Italian for
anything that must hold its shape unbaked.

## Push it further
Turn leftover Italian meringue into a baked Alaska. Then dry meringue kisses at 90°C for two
hours and use the crumbs as texture on a plated dessert.
