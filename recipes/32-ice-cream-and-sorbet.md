Almost every plated dessert has a frozen element, and a quenelle of good ice cream is worth
more on a plate than three fussy garnishes.

## Ingredients
**Ice cream:** 500ml whole milk · 250ml double cream · 1 vanilla pod · 6 egg yolks · 150g caster sugar · pinch salt

**Sorbet:** 500g fruit purée (raspberry, mango, blackcurrant) · 100–150g sugar · juice of half a lemon · 1 tbsp glucose or honey (optional, for texture)

## Method
1. **Crème anglaise.** Heat the milk, cream and split vanilla to a simmer. Whisk yolks with sugar and salt. Temper by pouring a third of the hot cream over the yolks while whisking, then return everything to the pan.
2. Cook over medium-low heat, stirring constantly with a spatula in a figure of eight, until it reaches **82–84°C** — *nappe*: it coats the back of the spoon and a finger drawn through leaves a clean line that does not run. Above 85°C it scrambles.
3. Strain, cool over ice, then **age it in the fridge overnight**. Ageing lets the fat crystallise and the proteins hydrate; the texture is noticeably smoother the next day.
4. Churn until it holds a soft ribbon, then freeze 4 hours to harden. Without a machine: freeze in a shallow tray and beat it hard with a fork every 30 minutes for 3 hours. Every beating breaks up ice crystals, and crystal size is the entire difference between silky and gritty.
5. **Sorbet.** Blend the fruit purée with sugar and lemon. The sugar level is not a taste decision alone — it controls texture. Too little and it freezes into a solid brick; too much and it never sets. If you have a refractometer aim for 26–28° Brix; otherwise use the old test: a fresh egg floated in the mix should show a coin-sized circle above the surface.
6. Churn or use the tray-and-fork method. Sorbet is best within two days.
7. **Quenelle:** dip a spoon in hot water, drag it across the surface while rolling your wrist, and turn out a smooth three-sided oval.

## What you're learning
Custard temperature control, and that frozen desserts are a physics problem: sugar depresses
the freezing point, fat and agitation control crystal size. Now you can explain why cheap
ice cream is icy.

## Push it further
Make a brown butter ice cream and a savoury one — sweetcorn, or olive oil. Then serve a
sorbet next to its own fresh fruit and see whether your version tastes more like the fruit
than the fruit does.
