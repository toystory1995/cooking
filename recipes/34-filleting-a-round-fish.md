Buy a whole sea bass, bream or mackerel. Filleting is the skill that lets you buy fish on
quality and price rather than on what has been trimmed for you.

## Ingredients
- 1 whole round fish, 600–800g, scaled and gutted
- Butter, oil, lemon, salt

## Method
1. **Prep.** Scale it in the sink or a bag — scales travel. Snip the fins off with scissors so nothing catches your knife. Dry it.
2. **First cut.** Lay the fish with its back towards you. Cut behind the gill plate down to the backbone, angling the knife towards the head so you do not waste the shoulder meat.
3. **Down the spine.** Turn the knife flat and cut along the backbone from head to tail in **long, confident strokes**, keeping the blade angled slightly down so you can feel the bones ticking under it. Short sawing cuts tear the flesh. Lift the fillet as it releases.
4. Over the ribcage, ride the blade along the bones rather than through them. Free the fillet at the tail.
5. Turn the fish over and repeat. The second side is always harder because the fish no longer sits flat — brace it with your hand.
6. **Pin-bone.** Run a fingertip up the centre line to find the pin bones, and pull each one out with tweezers **in the direction it points**, towards the head, or it will tear the flesh.
7. **Skin (optional).** Tail towards you, cut down to the skin, then hold the skin tight and run the knife along it at a shallow angle, moving the skin rather than the knife.
8. **Cook it** using recipe 07: dry, salt, hot pan, skin down, press flat, two-thirds up the side, flip, butter, baste.
9. **The frame** — head, bones, tail — makes fish stock in 20 minutes flat. Sweat aromatics, add the bones, cover with water and simmer **no longer than 20 minutes**; fish bones turn bitter after that.

## What you're learning
Knife angle and pressure against bone, and the fact that fish stock is on a completely
different clock from meat stock. You are also learning to judge freshness: clear eyes,
bright red gills, firm flesh, and it should smell of the sea and nothing else.

## Push it further
Do it three times in a week — the fish is cheap, and the third one is a different
experience from the first. Then try a flat fish, which comes off in four fillets and reads
the opposite way.
