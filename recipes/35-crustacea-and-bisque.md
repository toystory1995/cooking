Shellfish turns up constantly in competitions because it is expensive, technical, and most
people have never taken one apart. The rule that matters: the shells are not waste, they
are the sauce.

## Ingredients
- 1 live or freshly cooked brown crab, 1–1.5kg (or 500g langoustine or prawn shells)
- 1 onion, 1 carrot, 1 fennel bulb, 2 celery sticks — all roughly chopped
- 2 tbsp tomato purée, 2 tomatoes
- 100ml brandy, 200ml white wine
- 1.5 litres fish or light chicken stock
- 100ml double cream, 50g butter, cayenne, lemon
- Rice or a little flour to thicken (traditional)

## Method
1. **Dispatch humanely** if the crab is live: chill it in the freezer for 20 minutes to sedate it, then spike it through both nerve centres, or ask your fishmonger to do it. Boil 12 minutes per kilo, then cool.
2. **Pick it.** Twist off the claws and legs. Prise the body from the shell — push the underside up with your thumbs. Discard the **dead man's fingers**, the feathery grey gills, and the stomach sac behind the mouth. Everything else is edible: white meat from the body channels and claws, brown meat from inside the shell. Crack claws with the flat of a knife, not a hammer, or you drive shell into the meat. Pick the white meat over twice, feeling for shell fragments with your fingertips — a single shard on a plate is the whole dish gone.
3. **Bisque.** Break the shells up small and roast them at 220°C for 20 minutes until they smell sweet and toasted.
4. Sweat the vegetables in butter, add tomato purée and cook 2 minutes. Add the shells, then the brandy — flame it or reduce it — then the wine, reduced by half.
5. Add stock and a small handful of rice, and simmer **45 minutes only**. Longer and shellfish shells turn bitter and metallic, the same trap as fish bones.
6. Blitz briefly, then push through a fine sieve, pressing hard, then again through muslin. Reduce until it coats a spoon.
7. Finish off the heat with cream, butter, cayenne and lemon. Season carefully — it wants to be rich, sharp and faintly sweet.
8. Serve the bisque with a spoonful of the picked white meat and brown meat on toast alongside.

## What you're learning
Yield and extraction: one crab gives you a starter, a sauce and a stock, and knowing where
the meat hides is a genuine kitchen skill. Also that shellfish shells have their own short
cooking window, quite unlike bones.

## Push it further
Do the same with langoustines — pick the tails raw, roast the heads for the sauce. Then use
the bisque as the base for a risotto, or as the sauce for the egg yolk ravioli.
