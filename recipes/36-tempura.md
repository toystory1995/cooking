A batter with almost nothing in it, mixed badly on purpose, fried in a narrow temperature
window. Tempura is a technique dressed up as a dish.

## Ingredients
- 200g plain flour (or 150g flour + 50g cornflour), kept in the freezer
- 1 egg yolk
- 300ml **iced** sparkling water
- 1 litre neutral oil, plus 2 tbsp sesame oil
- Prawns, peeled with tails on; shiitake; sweet potato slices; shishito peppers; shiso leaves
- **Tentsuyu:** 200ml dashi · 50ml mirin · 50ml soy — simmered together; grated daikon and ginger to serve

## Method
1. **Prep everything first.** Once you start frying there is no time. Devein the prawns, then make three shallow cuts across the belly and press them straight — that stops them curling.
2. Heat the oil to **180°C**. Keep the thermometer in and watch it: each batch drops the temperature 10–20°C, and the recovery time between batches is the discipline of this dish.
3. **Batter, at the last possible moment.** Whisk the yolk into the iced water, then dump in the cold flour and stir **eight or nine times with chopsticks**. It must stay lumpy, with dry flour still visible. Every extra stir develops gluten, and gluten makes tempura chewy and heavy. Sit the bowl in a bowl of ice.
4. Dip a piece, let the excess drip, and lay it into the oil away from you. Fry four or five pieces at a time — never more.
5. Vegetables take 2–3 minutes; prawns 90 seconds. The sound tells you when they are done: a loud, urgent bubbling that settles into something quieter and drier as the water leaves.
6. Flick a few drops of batter from your chopsticks into the oil around each piece for the classic scattered *hana* petals.
7. Drain standing up on a rack. Salt immediately. Serve within a minute or two, with tentsuyu and grated daikon.
8. Skim the crumbs from the oil between batches or they burn and darken everything after them.

## What you're learning
Under-mixing as a technique, and the physics of a good fry: batter cold, oil hot, contact
brief, so water flashes to steam and leaves a lace shell instead of soaking in. This is the
same reason your samosa oil started low and finished high — different problems, same
variable.

## Push it further
Fry a shiso leaf battered on one side only, and a soft-boiled egg. Then measure what your
oil temperature actually does across six batches and adjust your heat ahead of the drop
rather than after it.
