The blistered, flaky samosa shell is not filo and not shortcrust — it is a stiff dough
rubbed with fat and fried from cool oil. Get the oil temperature wrong and it bubbles
smooth and greasy instead.

## Ingredients
**Pastry:** 300g plain flour · 4 tbsp ghee or oil · 1 tsp ajwain seeds · 1 tsp salt · about 120ml cold water

**Filling:** 500g potatoes, boiled and roughly crushed · 100g peas · 1 tsp cumin seeds · 1 tsp coriander seeds, crushed · 1 tsp garam masala · ½ tsp turmeric · 1 tbsp ginger · 2 green chillies · handful coriander · 1 tbsp amchoor or lemon juice · salt · 2 tbsp oil

## Method
1. **Pastry.** Rub the ghee into the flour with the ajwain and salt until it looks like wet sand and holds its shape when squeezed — this fat coating is what makes the shell flake. Add water sparingly to a **stiff**, dry dough. Knead just until smooth, then rest 30 minutes under a cloth. A soft dough gives you soft samosas.
2. **Filling.** Heat oil, add cumin and coriander seeds and wait for them to sizzle and darken — *tadka*, tempering. Add ginger and chilli, then the potato, peas and ground spices. Cook 5 minutes, then add amchoor, coriander and salt. Taste: it should be sour, hot and assertive, because pastry mutes everything. Cool completely.
3. **Shape.** Roll a golf ball of dough into a 16cm oval, then cut it in half. Take one half, brush the straight edge with water, and fold it into a cone, overlapping the straight edges by 1cm. Press that seam **hard**.
4. Fill the cone two-thirds full. Press the filling down, brush the open rim with water, and seal the top edge flat, pressing out the air. Stand it up: it should sit on its own three corners.
5. **Fry.** Heat oil to just **140°C** and fry the samosas slowly for 12–15 minutes, turning, until pale gold and blistered. Then lift them out, bring the oil to 180°C and fry again for 2 minutes to deepen the colour. Low first, hot second — that is what gives the bubbled, flaky shell.
6. Drain on a rack, not paper. Eat with tamarind and mint chutneys.

## What you're learning
Two-stage frying, and how fat rubbed into flour before water creates flakiness by getting
in the way of gluten — the same principle as shortcrust, working in the opposite direction
from the lamination in croissants.

## Push it further
Make a lamb keema filling, which has fat and moisture the potato does not, and adjust the
seal accordingly. Bake a tray as a comparison and taste what frying actually contributes.
