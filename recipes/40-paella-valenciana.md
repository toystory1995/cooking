The exact opposite of risotto: nothing is stirred, the rice must stay separate and dry, and
the prize is the caramelised crust on the bottom. Two rice dishes, two contradictory sets
of rules — which is the point of cooking them close together.

## Ingredients
- 300g bomba or calasparra rice
- 900ml chicken or rabbit stock, hot (a 1:3 ratio by volume, rice to stock)
- 400g chicken thigh and/or rabbit, in small pieces
- 200g green beans, 100g butter beans
- **Sofrito:** 2 tomatoes grated · 1 onion very finely diced · 4 cloves garlic · 1 tsp sweet paprika
- Good pinch saffron, toasted and crushed
- 4 tbsp olive oil, salt, rosemary sprig
- Lemon to serve

## Method
1. **Get the pan wide.** Paella is a thin layer of rice — 1.5cm at most — cooked by evaporation. A deep pan makes rice pudding. If your burner is smaller than the pan, plan to move it around, or cook it over coals.
2. Brown the meat in oil around the edge of the pan, in batches, until properly coloured. Push it out to the rim.
3. **Sofrito.** In the centre, cook the onion slowly until soft, add garlic, then the grated tomato and paprika. Cook it down until dark, jammy and the oil separates — 15 minutes. This is the flavour base and it cannot be rushed.
4. Add the beans, then the rice, and stir it through the sofrito for **one minute only**. This is the last time you stir anything.
5. Pour in the hot stock with the saffron, spread everything evenly with the back of a spoon, and level the rice. Add the rosemary. Now **do not touch it again**.
6. High heat for 10 minutes, then medium-low for 8–10 more, turning the pan for even heat. Taste a grain at the edge from minute 16.
7. **Socarrat.** When the liquid is gone and the rice is just done, turn the heat back to high for **60–90 seconds**. Listen: the pan goes from a bubbling sound to a dry crackle, and you will smell toasting — the moment before burning. That crust on the base is the best part of the dish.
8. Rest, covered with a cloth, for 5 minutes. Serve from the pan with lemon.

## What you're learning
Rice cooked by evaporation rather than absorption-with-agitation, and the confidence to
leave a pan alone. Cook this within a month of the risotto and the contrast will teach you
more about starch than either alone.

## Push it further
Try a seafood paella, where the timing is much tighter because the shellfish must go in
late. Then attempt one over a wood fire, which is how it is meant to be done.
