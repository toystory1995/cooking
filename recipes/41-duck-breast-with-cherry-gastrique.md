Duck rewards patience twice: once in the fat rendering, once in the sauce. This is the
first dish in the library that would not look out of place in a restaurant.

## Ingredients
- 2 duck breasts, skin on
- **Gastrique:** 60g sugar · 60ml red wine vinegar · 150ml brown chicken stock · 150g cherries, halved and stoned · knob of butter · thyme
- Salt, pepper

## Method
1. Score the skin in a 1cm diamond pattern, cutting through the fat but **not** into the flesh. Salt it and leave uncovered in the fridge for an hour if you can.
2. Lay the breasts skin-down in a **cold, dry** pan and put it over low-medium heat. Starting cold gives the fat time to render out before the skin sets — the whole point of the dish.
3. Render for 12–15 minutes, pouring off the fat two or three times into a jar (keep it — best roast potatoes you will make). The skin should be thin, flat and deep amber.
4. Flip, cook 2–4 minutes to **52°C** at the centre. Rest on a rack, skin up, for 10 minutes; it will carry over to about 56°C, medium-rare.
5. **Gastrique.** In a clean pan, melt the sugar dry over medium heat to a mahogany caramel. Stand back and pour in the vinegar — it will spit and seize; keep stirring until it dissolves again.
6. Add the stock and thyme and reduce until it coats a spoon. Add the cherries for the last 2 minutes so they hold their shape. Whisk in cold butter off the heat, and add any resting juices.
7. Carve across the breast into 1cm slices, fan them out skin-side up, and spoon the sauce around. Season the cut surfaces with flaky salt at the last second.

## What you're learning
A gastrique is caramel plus acid: sweet-sour balance built from scratch, which is how you
cut through fat without reaching for a jar of chutney. You are also learning that fat
renders on time and temperature, not on heat alone.

## Push it further
Swap cherries for blood orange in winter, or rhubarb in spring. Then try the same
cold-pan render on a chicken supreme and see how much slower duck skin needs to be.
