*Hane* — the crisp lace wing that joins the dumplings into one sheet. It is a starch
slurry and a nerve test, because you cannot look at it while it works.

## Ingredients
- 300g pork mince
- 200g cabbage, very finely chopped, salted and squeezed **bone dry**
- 2 spring onions, 1 tbsp ginger, 2 cloves garlic
- 1 tbsp soy, 1 tbsp sake, 2 tsp sesame oil, ½ tsp sugar, salt
- 30 round gyoza wrappers
- **Skirt slurry:** 1 tsp plain flour or potato starch dissolved in 150ml water
- Neutral oil; for dipping: soy, rice vinegar, chilli oil

## Method
1. Salt the chopped cabbage, wait 15 minutes, then wring it out in a cloth with real force. Watery cabbage is the reason most home gyoza are soggy.
2. Mix the filling and beat until it holds together. Chill 30 minutes so it firms up and is easier to portion.
3. Fill each wrapper with 2 teaspoons. Wet the front half of the rim only. Fold and, holding the back edge flat, make **six or seven pleats on the near side only**, pressing each into the flat back. Sit each finished gyoza down so it flattens its own base.
4. Heat a thin film of oil in the pan. Arrange the gyoza in a tight circle or rows, touching — they must touch, or the skirt has nothing to join.
5. Fry 2 minutes until the bases are golden. Now pour in the slurry, straight to the edges, and clamp on the lid. Steam 6–7 minutes.
6. Take the lid off and let every drop of water evaporate. Keep going past the point of nerves: the starch left behind turns from white slop to a thin translucent sheet to golden lace, and that takes another 2–4 minutes. Listen for the frying sound to sharpen.
7. Loosen the edge with a spatula, put a plate over the pan and invert in one movement. Serve skirt-up.

## What you're learning
That water leaves in stages and starch only crisps once it has all gone. The same
observation drives the potsticker, the crackling on pork and the base of a good pizza.

## Push it further
Make a prawn-and-shiso version, and one vegetarian with shiitake and firm tofu, where you
must add fat back in to compensate for the missing pork.
