Two ingredients, no yeast, and a batter that rises on wild bacteria alone. Then the
difficult part: spreading it paper-thin on a pan at exactly the right heat.

## Ingredients
**Batter:** 300g parboiled rice (or basmati at a push) · 100g urad dal (skinned black gram) · 1 tsp fenugreek seeds · water · salt

**Potato masala:** 4 potatoes boiled and crushed · 1 onion sliced · 1 tsp mustard seeds · 1 tsp urad dal · 10 curry leaves · 1 tsp turmeric · 2 green chillies · ginger · salt

**Coconut chutney:** 100g fresh coconut · 2 tbsp roasted chana dal · 1 green chilli · small piece ginger · salt · water — plus a tempering of mustard seeds, curry leaves and a dried chilli in hot oil

## Method
1. **Soak** the rice separately from the dal and fenugreek, 6 hours.
2. **Grind** the dal with the fenugreek first, adding cold water slowly, until it is very smooth, white and **aerated** — it should float when dropped in water. Then grind the rice to a slightly grainy paste. Combine by hand: your hands carry the wild organisms that start the ferment.
3. Add salt, cover loosely, and leave in a warm place **8–16 hours**. It is ready when it has risen visibly, is full of bubbles and smells sour and yeasty. In a cold kitchen this takes far longer — use the oven with the light on.
4. **Masala.** Temper mustard seeds and urad dal in oil until they pop, add curry leaves, chilli and ginger, then onion, turmeric and the crushed potato. Season and keep warm.
5. **Chutney.** Blend the coconut, chana dal, chilli and ginger with water to a thick sauce. Temper the spices in hot oil and pour over.
6. **Cook the dosa.** Heat the tawa to medium-high. Test it: flick water on — it should skitter and vanish in about two seconds. Wipe the pan with a halved onion dipped in oil (this seasons and cools it evenly).
7. Pour a ladle in the centre and spread it outward in a continuous spiral with the base of the ladle, quickly and lightly. Drizzle oil round the edge. Cook 2 minutes until the base is lacy and deep gold and the edges lift.
8. Do **not** flip a thin dosa. Add masala, fold, and serve immediately with chutney.

## What you're learning
Wild fermentation without a starter, plus the fine art of pan temperature: too cool and the
batter sticks and steams; too hot and it seizes before you can spread it. The first three
dosas of any session are practice — every South Indian cook knows this.

## Push it further
Use the same batter for uttapam (thick, with toppings) and idli (steamed). Then leave the
batter three days and taste how much more sour it gets.
