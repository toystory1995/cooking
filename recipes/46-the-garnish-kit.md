The components restaurant kitchens have in their fridge all week, so that a plate takes
four minutes to build. Make these once, keep them, and everything you cook afterwards looks
finished.

## Ingredients
**Herb oil:** 100g parsley, chive or basil (leaves only) · 200ml neutral oil

**Powders:** mushroom, olive, beetroot skins, herb stalks, shrimp shells — anything you would otherwise bin

**Crumb:** 100g stale sourdough · 50g butter · garlic · lemon zest · anchovy (optional)

**Quick gel:** 250ml of any flavourful liquid — apple juice, dashi, beetroot juice · 2g agar agar

## Method
1. **Herb oil.** Blanch the herbs for 15 seconds, then straight into iced water and squeeze bone dry — blanching sets the chlorophyll so the oil stays vivid green instead of turning khaki within a day. Blend hard with the oil for 2 minutes until warm and bright, then hang through muslin **without squeezing**, in the fridge, for a few hours. It should drip through crystal clear and intensely green.
2. **Powders.** Dry the ingredient completely at 60–70°C in a low oven with the door ajar, or in a dehydrator: mushroom slices 6 hours, olives 4, herb stalks 3. Blitz to a powder and sieve. Store with a silica sachet or a spoon of rice in the jar. A pinch of mushroom powder salted 1:4 is one of the most useful seasonings you will own.
3. **Crumb.** Blitz the bread coarse, fry slowly in butter with garlic and anchovy until deeply golden and completely dry, then finish with lemon zest off the heat. Cool spread out, store airtight. This is the texture element that saves a soft plate.
4. **Agar gel.** Whisk the agar into the cold liquid, bring to a full boil for **2 minutes** — agar only activates above 90°C — then pour into a tray and chill. It sets firm in 20 minutes. Cut into cubes, or blend the set gel into a smooth **fluid gel** that pipes into dots and holds its shape without running.
5. Store everything labelled and dated. Oils and gels keep a week, powders and crumbs a month.

## What you're learning
That "modern" plating is mostly preparation, not trickery, and that agar behaves completely
differently from gelatine: it sets at room temperature, holds up to heat, and gives a
brittle set you can blend into a fluid gel.

## Push it further
Add a foam using lecithin, a pickled element and a purée, and you have a full component
library. Then set yourself a rule: every plate this month gets exactly one of them, no more.
