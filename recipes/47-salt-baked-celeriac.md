One vegetable, three preparations, one plate. This kind of thinking — take an ingredient
apart and show it in several forms — is exactly what an invention test rewards.

## Ingredients
- 2 celeriac, scrubbed
- **Salt crust:** 1kg coarse salt · 300g plain flour · 4 egg whites · 150ml water · thyme, crushed juniper
- 200ml double cream, 50g butter, milk
- Brown butter, hazelnuts, apple, lemon, chives

## Method
1. **Crust.** Mix salt, flour, herbs, egg whites and water into a stiff paste like wet sand. Spread a bed on a tray, sit one whole celeriac on it, and pack the rest over the top in an even 1cm layer, sealing completely. There must be no gaps — the crust works by making a sealed chamber.
2. Bake at 200°C for **90 minutes**. A skewer through the crust should slide through the celeriac with no resistance.
3. Meanwhile peel and dice the second celeriac. **Purée:** cook it gently in a covered pan with butter, a little milk and salt until completely soft, then blend with cream until glass-smooth and pass through a sieve. It should hold a line on the plate but drop off a spoon.
4. **Crisps or a raw element:** slice a few thin discs of the raw celeriac on a mandoline and either fry them at 160°C until pale gold, or dress them raw with lemon and oil for a sharp, crunchy contrast.
5. Crack the crust open at the table — it comes away in plates. Wipe the celeriac, peel the skin off, and cut into thick wedges. It will be sweet, dense and seasoned right through without ever touching the salt.
6. Plate: a sweep of purée, wedges of baked celeriac glazed in brown butter, crisps or raw slivers on top, toasted hazelnuts, a squeeze of lemon, chives.

## What you're learning
The salt crust as a cooking vessel: it insulates, holds moisture and seasons through the
skin by vapour rather than contact. And the discipline of one ingredient in three textures,
which reads as intent on a plate rather than decoration.

## Push it further
Salt-bake a beetroot, a whole sea bass and a chicken. Each behaves differently, and the
fish is the one worth mastering — it is a genuine restaurant showpiece.
