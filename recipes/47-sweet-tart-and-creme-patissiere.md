The two building blocks of a pastry section. Once you can make a crisp shell and a smooth
pastry cream, half the patisserie counter is available to you.

## Ingredients
**Pâte sucrée:** 250g plain flour · 150g cold butter, diced · 90g icing sugar · 30g ground almonds · 1 egg · pinch salt

**Crème pâtissière:** 500ml whole milk · 1 vanilla pod · 6 egg yolks · 120g caster sugar · 45g cornflour · 40g butter

**To finish:** 500g fruit — strawberries, raspberries, figs, apricots · 3 tbsp apricot jam warmed with a splash of water

## Method
1. **Pastry.** Rub the butter into the flour, sugar, almonds and salt to fine crumbs — or pulse in a processor. Add the egg and bring together **without kneading**. Flatten into a disc, wrap, chill 1 hour. Overworked sweet pastry shrinks and toughens.
2. Roll to 3mm between two sheets of baking paper, which stops it sticking and needing extra flour. Line the ring, pressing well into the corner without stretching, and leave the excess hanging over the edge. Chill 30 minutes — **cold pastry does not shrink**.
3. **Blind bake** at 170°C with paper and beans for 20 minutes, then remove the beans and bake 10–12 more until evenly sandy-gold across the base. Trim the overhang with a small knife while hot. Brush the inside with a little beaten egg and return for 2 minutes to seal it against the filling.
4. **Crème pâtissière.** Infuse the milk with the split vanilla pod. Whisk yolks, sugar and cornflour to a smooth paste. Pour a third of the hot milk over, whisking, then return everything to the pan.
5. Cook over medium heat, whisking **constantly and into the corners**, until it thickens. Then keep going: it must **boil for a full minute** or the cornflour stays raw and the cream will thin out as it sits. It will look alarmingly thick; that is right.
6. Off the heat, beat in the butter. Pour into a tray, film pressed directly on the surface, and chill fast. Before using, beat it smooth again.
7. Fill the shell, level it, and arrange the fruit in tight concentric rings from the outside in. Brush with warm jam for shine.
8. Assemble within 2 hours of serving, or the base softens.

## What you're learning
Two opposite jobs of starch and fat: fat shortening the pastry to make it crumble, starch
thickening the cream and needing heat to do it. Also blind baking, which is a lesson in
patience — most soggy bottoms are simply underbaked shells.

## Push it further
Turn the same cream into crème diplomate (folded with whipped cream) and crème mousseline
(beaten with butter), then use them in a mille-feuille.
