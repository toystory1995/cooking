Offal turns up in competitions constantly, because it is cheap, technical and divides
people. A parfait is the most persuasive thing you can make from it.

## Ingredients
- 500g chicken livers, trimmed
- 250g butter, softened, plus 100g clarified for the top
- 3 eggs
- 2 shallots, sliced
- 100ml Madeira, 100ml port, 50ml brandy
- 1 clove garlic, thyme
- 10g salt, pink curing salt optional for colour
- Toasted brioche and a sharp chutney, to serve

## Method
1. **Trim** the livers properly: cut away every green-tinged patch (bile — bitter) and the connective threads between the lobes. Soak them in milk for an hour if they smell strong, then drain and dry.
2. Reduce the shallots, garlic, thyme and all three alcohols in a pan until **almost dry and syrupy**. Cool. This is where the flavour concentration comes from; leaving liquid behind makes a loose parfait.
3. Blend the livers, eggs, the reduction and salt until completely smooth. With the motor running, add the softened butter in pieces until glossy and emulsified.
4. **Pass it through a fine sieve.** Push it through with a ladle and discard what will not go. This one step is the whole difference between rustic pâté and silky parfait.
5. Pour into a terrine lined with cling film or into ramekins. Tap out the air bubbles.
6. **Cook in a water bath**: hot water to two-thirds up the terrine, in a 130°C oven, until the centre reads **63–65°C**, about 45–55 minutes. Above 70°C the eggs curdle and it turns grainy and grey — this is the same egg-protein window as your hollandaise.
7. Cool, then chill for at least 6 hours. Pour clarified butter over the top to seal it — a proper seal will keep it a week and stops the surface oxidising grey.
8. Serve cold with hot brioche, flaky salt and something acidic.

## What you're learning
Gentle, precise cooking of an egg-bound emulsion, and the fact that "passing it" is not
fussiness — it is the difference between good and very good. Also, trimming: with offal,
the preparation is most of the outcome.

## Push it further
Try a coarse country pâté with the same livers, and then the harder version: sweetbreads,
blanched, pressed, peeled and crisped in butter.
