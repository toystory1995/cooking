Sous-vide is just holding a temperature. You do not need the machine to learn what it
teaches: that every protein has a temperature at which it becomes what you want.

## Ingredients
- 4 eggs
- 2 chicken breasts, skin on
- 4 egg yolks and 300ml olive oil (for confit yolks)
- 400g salmon fillet
- Salt, aromatics

## Method
1. **The technique.** Fill a large pot with water and bring it to a few degrees above your target. Take it off the heat, add the food, put the lid on and wrap it in a towel — or use a cool box, which holds a temperature astonishingly well. Check every 10 minutes and add a splash of boiling water if it drops. You are aiming to stay within ±2°C.
2. **63°C egg**, 45 minutes: the white just barely set and silky, the yolk thick and pourable like a sauce. At 65°C the white firms; at 68°C the yolk starts to set. Cook three at different temperatures and crack them side by side — this single comparison teaches more about eggs than any recipe.
3. **Chicken breast at 62°C**, 1 hour, bagged in a zip-lock with the air pressed out under water. It comes out disconcertingly pale, so finish it hard in a hot pan skin-side down for 90 seconds. The texture is completely different from a roasted breast.
4. **Salmon at 45°C**, 25 minutes: translucent, buttery, falling into fat flakes. Anything above 50°C and you are back to conventional cooked fish.
5. **Confit yolks**: yolks slipped into olive oil held at 63°C for 45 minutes. They set to a soft, rich sphere you can lift whole onto a plate.
6. Keep notes of your temperatures and times. That notebook becomes your own reference.

## The temperatures worth memorising
- **50°C** fish begins to set · **54°C** medium-rare beef · **60°C** chicken safe if held 12 minutes
- **63°C** egg yolk thickens · **65°C** hollandaise ceiling · **70°C** egg white fully set
- **74°C** poultry instant-safe · **85°C** collagen begins converting in earnest

## What you're learning
That "doneness" is a temperature, not a duration, and that safety is a combination of
temperature *and* time — 60°C held for twelve minutes is as safe for chicken as 74°C for an
instant. This is the knowledge that lets you cook confidently rather than defensively.

## Push it further
Hold a duck leg in its own fat at 80°C for 8 hours for a true confit. Then run the same
chicken breast at 60, 62 and 65°C and decide, on your own palate, which one you would serve.
