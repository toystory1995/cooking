Dough with a third of its weight in butter. Fat coats gluten strands and stops them
joining, so the whole technique is a sequence problem: build the structure first, then
persuade the butter into it.

## Ingredients
- 500g strong white flour
- 60g caster sugar
- 10g fine salt
- 10g instant yeast
- 6 eggs (about 300g), cold
- 250g butter, cold but pliable, cubed
- 1 egg beaten with a splash of milk, for washing

## Method
1. Put the flour, sugar, yeast and salt in the mixer — salt on one side, yeast on the other so they do not touch directly. Add the eggs and mix on low until it comes together.
2. **Now knead for 8–10 minutes** on medium until the dough is smooth, elastic and slaps cleanly off the side of the bowl. It should pass a windowpane test: a piece stretched thin enough to see light through without tearing. **Do not add the butter before this point** — do it early and the dough will never develop strength.
3. Add the butter a few cubes at a time, waiting until each addition disappears. It will look broken and greasy in the middle of this and come back together. 10 more minutes. The finished dough is glossy, very slack and clings to everything.
4. **First prove** 1 hour at room temperature, then knock back and refrigerate **overnight, 8–12 hours**. Cold brioche is the only workable brioche — warm, it is unshapeable soup.
5. Shape cold and fast: for a loaf, divide into six balls, roll each tight, and drop them into a lined tin in two rows. For individual *brioche à tête*, roll a ball, pinch off a smaller head and seat it in a dimple on top.
6. **Second prove** 2–3 hours at room temperature until nearly doubled and wobbling. Watch the butter — if the tin looks greasy, the room is too warm.
7. Egg wash twice, gently, avoiding the sides of the tin. Bake at 180°C for 30–35 minutes, tenting with foil if it darkens early, to an internal 90°C.
8. Cool completely in the tin for 10 minutes, then out onto a rack.

## What you're learning
Order of operations. Gluten first, fat second — the same reasoning that makes you rub fat
into flour *before* water for shortcrust, applied in reverse. It also gives you the dough
behind doughnuts, babka, cinnamon buns and the bun for a serious burger.

## Push it further
Use a third of the dough for a chocolate babka, and stale slices for the best bread and
butter pudding you will make. Then push to 400g butter per kilo of flour and see where it
stops working.
