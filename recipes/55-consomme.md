The dish that proves a cook's discipline. There is no garnish, no sauce, nowhere to hide —
just liquid you should be able to read a newspaper through.

## Ingredients
- 2 litres cold brown chicken stock, thoroughly degreased (see recipe 06)
- **Clarification raft:** 400g lean minced beef or chicken · 4 egg whites · 1 onion, 1 carrot, 1 celery stick, all finely chopped · 2 tomatoes chopped · few parsley stalks and thyme
- Salt, sherry or Madeira to finish

## Method
1. Chill the stock completely and lift off every trace of fat. Fat is the enemy of clarity.
2. Mix the raft ingredients together and combine them with the **cold** stock in the pot. Everything must start cold, or the whites set before they can do their job.
3. Bring it up slowly over medium-low heat, stirring gently and constantly until it reaches about 50°C. Then **stop stirring completely**.
4. As it heats, the proteins coagulate and rise, dragging every particle of cloudiness with them, and form a solid grey raft on the surface. Do not disturb it.
5. When the raft has set, make a small hole in it with a ladle to let steam escape and to give you a window to work through. Hold the pot at the barest simmer — 85–90°C, no bubbles breaking except through that hole — for 60–90 minutes.
6. Ladle the clear liquid out through the hole, never tipping the pot, and pass it through a double layer of damp muslin. Do not press.
7. Season carefully at the end and add a spoon of Madeira. It should taste more of chicken than chicken does.

## What you're learning
Protein coagulation as a filter, and the outer limit of gentle heat control — the same
principle as the omelette, the poached fish and the hollandaise, taken to its extreme.

## Push it further
Serve it with a single garnish: a tortellini, a poached quail egg, a brunoise of vegetables
cut into 2mm dice. Cutting a clean brunoise is knife practice that shows on the plate.
