Three elements, one plate, everything hot at once. From here on, timing is as much of a
skill as cooking.

## Ingredients
- 1 rack of lamb, 6–8 bones
- 2 large potatoes, cut into 5cm cylinders
- 200g frozen peas, 30g butter, mint
- 100g butter, 200ml chicken stock, garlic, thyme, rosemary
- Dijon, breadcrumbs and parsley for a crust (optional)
- Oil, salt, pepper

## Method
1. **Trim the rack.** Score the fat cap in a diamond. Cut down between the bone tips and scrape each bone completely clean with the back of a knife — French trimming. Save the trimmings and roast them for the sauce.
2. **Fondant potatoes.** Trim the cylinders flat on both ends. Colour them in foaming butter in an oven-safe pan, one cut face at a time, until deep gold. Add garlic and thyme, then pour in stock to come halfway up. Into a 190°C oven for 30–35 minutes, basting once, until a knife slides through and the stock has reduced to a glaze.
3. **Pea purée.** Boil the peas for 2 minutes in salted water, drain, and blend immediately with butter, mint and a splash of the water until completely smooth. Push it through a sieve. Keep it warm and covered — it dulls from bright green in about twenty minutes, so make it late.
4. **The lamb.** Season and sear fat-side down in a hot pan until well coloured, then all other faces. Roast at 190°C for 12–15 minutes to **52°C** at the centre. Rest 10 minutes; it will climb to 56°C.
5. If crusting, brush the fat with Dijon and press on breadcrumbs mixed with parsley before the last 5 minutes in the oven.
6. **Plate.** Sweep the purée with the back of a spoon, sit two fondants alongside, carve the rack into double cutlets and lean them against the potato with the bones up. Sauce around, not over. Wipe the rim.

## Method note on timing
Work backwards from the plate: potatoes in the oven first, lamb in 30 minutes later, purée
while the lamb rests, plates warming throughout. Write the order down before you start.

## What you're learning
Multi-element service. Each component is straightforward; the skill is choreography, and a
written timeline is what professionals actually rely on.

## Push it further
Add a fourth element — glazed carrots, or a lamb jus made from those roasted trimmings —
without letting anything else slip. Then take one away and ask whether the plate is better.
