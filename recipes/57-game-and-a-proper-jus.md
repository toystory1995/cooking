Game appears in every competition autumn, and it frightens people because it is lean, dark
and unforgiving. It is also where the difference between a *sauce* and a *jus* becomes
obvious, because there is nothing else on the plate to hide behind.

## Ingredients
- 2 whole wood pigeons (or 1 mallard, or 2 partridge), plucked and drawn
- 200g chicken wings, chopped, to bulk the jus
- 1 shallot, 1 carrot, 2 cloves garlic, thyme, 6 juniper berries, 1 bay
- 100ml red wine, 50ml port, 500ml brown chicken stock
- 30g cold butter, 1 tsp sherry vinegar
- Celeriac purée (see recipe 43), blackberries or a little redcurrant jelly
- Butter, oil, salt

## Method
1. **Prepare the birds.** Remove the legs and wings; keep the crowns whole. Chop the legs, wings and backs for the jus — game bones are where the flavour is. Check for shot with your fingers as you go.
2. **Jus.** Brown the chopped game bones and chicken wings hard in a wide pan, in batches, until genuinely dark — this is a jus, so the colour *is* the flavour. Add the vegetables and aromatics, then the wine and port, and reduce almost to a glaze, scraping the base.
3. Add the stock and simmer gently for **90 minutes**, skimming. Strain through a fine sieve, then reduce until it is glossy and coats a spoon — a proper jus should be thin enough to run but leave a line on the plate. Never thicken it with flour. Finish off the heat with cold butter and a few drops of vinegar to cut the richness.
4. **The legs**, being tough, want the opposite treatment: confit them slowly in duck fat at 80°C for 2 hours, or braise them in a little of the jus. Never roast them alongside the crown.
5. **Roast the crowns.** Season and brown in foaming butter on both breasts and the back, basting. Then into a 200°C oven for **6–8 minutes only**, to **52°C** at the breast. Pigeon and duck are served pink; taken to well-done they turn liver-like and dry.
6. **Rest 10 minutes**, breast down, in a warm place. This is a long rest for a small bird and it matters more here than in a chicken.
7. Carve the breasts off the crown in one piece each. Plate on the purée with the confit leg, blackberries, and the jus spooned around.

## What you're learning
Two cuts from one animal cooked in opposite directions, and a proper jus: bones, browning,
reduction and butter, with no thickener anywhere. Once you can make one, every roast you
cook afterwards improves.

## Push it further
Try venison loin the same way — hard sear, 52°C, long rest — with the same jus and a
juniper and chocolate note. Then hang a bird a few extra days if your butcher will let you,
and taste what ageing does.
