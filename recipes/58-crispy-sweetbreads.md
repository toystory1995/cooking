Sweetbreads turn up in competitions because they are technical, unfamiliar and delicious
when handled properly — and grey, spongy and off-putting when not. The difference is four
preparation steps that most people skip.

## Ingredients
- 500g lamb or veal sweetbreads (the heart-shaped throat ones if you can choose)
- Milk or salted water for soaking
- Court bouillon: water, a splash of white wine, bay, peppercorns, lemon, carrot, onion
- 50g plain flour, salt, pepper
- 60g butter, 2 tbsp capers, handful of sage, juice of half a lemon
- To serve: pea purée, or a sharp leaf salad

## Method
1. **Soak** the sweetbreads in several changes of cold salted water or milk for 3–4 hours in the fridge, until the water runs clear and they look pale ivory rather than pink. This draws out blood, and skipping it is why badly prepared sweetbreads taste metallic.
2. **Blanch.** Bring the court bouillon to a simmer, slide them in, and poach very gently for **4–5 minutes** — no bubbling. Lift them straight into iced water.
3. **Peel.** While still cool and damp, pull away the outer membrane and any hard fatty threads with your fingers and the tip of a knife. Work patiently; the membrane shrinks in the pan and makes them tough and curled if it is left on. Separate them into natural walnut-sized lobes.
4. **Press.** Lay them between two trays lined with kitchen paper, weight lightly and refrigerate for at least an hour, ideally overnight. Pressing gives an even thickness that browns uniformly, and a firmer, meatier bite.
5. **Cook.** Dry them thoroughly. Season, then dust lightly in flour and knock off the excess — a heavy coating steams, a light one crisps.
6. Fry in a little oil in a hot pan for 2–3 minutes a side, undisturbed, until deep golden and crusted. Add the butter, sage and capers, let the butter foam and brown, and baste for 30 seconds.
7. Finish with lemon off the heat. Serve immediately on pea purée, with the brown butter, capers and crisp sage spooned over.

## What you're learning
That offal is a preparation discipline more than a cooking one: soak, blanch, peel, press.
Once those four are habit, sweetbreads, brains, tripe and kidneys all become approachable —
and they are the cheapest way to cook something genuinely luxurious.

## Push it further
Try devilled kidneys, which need the opposite treatment — fast, hot and barely pink. Then
serve sweetbreads to someone without telling them what they are, and ask afterwards.
