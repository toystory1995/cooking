*Raviolo al uovo*: cut into it and the yolk should run out as sauce. Ninety seconds too
long and you have made an expensive dumpling.

## Ingredients
- 1 batch fresh egg pasta (recipe 10), rested
- 250g ricotta, drained overnight
- 50g Parmesan, grated
- 100g spinach, wilted, squeezed bone dry, chopped
- 4 very fresh egg yolks, separated with the shell so none breaks
- Nutmeg, salt, pepper
- 80g butter, sage leaves, extra Parmesan

## Method
1. Beat the ricotta with Parmesan, spinach, nutmeg and plenty of seasoning until smooth. Put it in a piping bag. It must be thick enough to hold a wall.
2. Roll the pasta to the **thinnest setting** — genuinely translucent. Work with one sheet at a time and keep the rest covered; dry pasta will not seal.
3. On a lightly floured sheet, pipe a ring of filling about 7cm across, with a wall high enough to cradle a yolk. Make four.
4. Slide one yolk into the centre of each ring. Handle them like they are made of glass; a broken yolk cannot be rescued.
5. Brush around each ring with water. Lay a second sheet over the top and, starting from the centre and working outwards, press the air out and seal. **Trapped air expands and bursts the parcel in the water.**
6. Cut with the larger cutter and press the edges again. Rest on semolina.
7. Brown the butter with sage. Have the plates warm and everything else finished — this dish waits for nobody.
8. Cook the ravioli in gently simmering salted water for exactly **90 seconds** — a rolling boil will tear them. Lift out with a slotted spoon, drain on a cloth, plate, spoon over brown butter, grate Parmesan, add black pepper.

## What you're learning
Precision under time pressure, and the habit of finishing every other component before the
one element that cannot wait. This is the mental model that gets people through a pressure
test.

## Push it further
Make eight and serve four, so you have room to lose two. That is not defeatism, it is how
kitchens plan. Then try the same construction with a confit egg yolk cooked at 63°C first.
