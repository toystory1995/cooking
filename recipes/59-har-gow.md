The dim sum chef's exam. Translucent skin, ten or more pleats, and a filling that snaps.
The dough is unlike anything else you have handled: no gluten at all, so it tears rather
than stretches, and it only behaves while it is hot.

## Ingredients
**Wrappers:** 100g wheat starch · 40g tapioca starch · ½ tsp salt · 160ml boiling water · 1 tbsp neutral oil

**Filling:** 300g raw prawns · 50g pork fat or fatty pork belly, finely diced · 50g bamboo shoots, finely diced · 1 tsp sugar · ½ tsp salt · 1 tsp sesame oil · 1 tbsp tapioca starch · white pepper

## Method
1. **Filling.** Chop a third of the prawns to a paste and the rest into rough 1cm pieces — the paste binds, the pieces give bite. Mix with everything else and chill for an hour. The starch and the chilling are what make the filling set into a springy plug rather than a wet mass.
2. **Dough.** Whisk the starches and salt together. Pour on the **fully boiling** water all at once, stirring hard, then cover and leave for 2 minutes. Add the oil and knead on the bench while it is almost too hot to touch, until smooth and putty-like. Anything less than boiling water and it will never come together.
3. Keep the dough in a covered bowl and work with one piece at a time. It dries and cracks within a minute in the open air.
4. Roll 10g pieces into balls. Flatten each with the oiled flat of a cleaver, pressing and dragging in one smooth motion, into a 9cm translucent disc. It takes practice; a small rolling pin between two oiled sheets also works.
5. Fill with a heaped teaspoon. Fold in half and pleat the **near side only** — aim for ten to twelve small, even pleats pressed into the flat back — then curve the dumpling into a crescent.
6. Steam on oiled paper over a hard boil for exactly **6–7 minutes**. Overcooked, the skin goes gummy and slumps; undercooked, it is chalky and opaque.
7. Serve immediately in the steamer. They are at their best for about four minutes.

## What you're learning
A gluten-free, starch-gelatinised dough — completely different physics from every dough so
far, which is why it demands heat and speed. Also portion discipline: at this size, a gram
of extra filling shows.

## Push it further
Make *siu mai* with the same filling logic in a wonton wrapper, then serve both with a
third steamer of cheung fun. Building a three-item dim sum service is a genuine timing
exercise.
