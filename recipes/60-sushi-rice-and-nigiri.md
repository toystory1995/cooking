Sushi is judged on the rice, not the fish. Ten years of apprenticeship goes into what looks
like a small oblong of nothing.

## Ingredients
- 300g Japanese short-grain rice
- 330ml water
- **Seasoning:** 60ml rice vinegar · 30g sugar · 8g salt, warmed until dissolved
- 250g sashimi-grade fish: salmon, tuna, sea bream
- Nori, wasabi, soy, pickled ginger

## Method
1. **Wash the rice** in cold water, swirling with your fingers and pouring off, five or six times, until the water runs almost clear. Drain in a sieve for 20 minutes. Excess surface starch is what makes sushi rice gluey.
2. Cook with the measured water: bring to a boil, lid on, lowest heat 12 minutes, then **rest off the heat, lid on, for 10 minutes**. Never lift the lid.
3. Tip the hot rice into a wide wooden or plastic tray — not metal, and never deep. Pour the seasoning over a spatula so it disperses.
4. **Cut, don't stir.** Slice through the rice at an angle with the edge of the spatula, folding gently, while fanning it. You are cooling it to body temperature and coating each grain, not mashing them. Finished *shari* is glossy, at 35–37°C, and each grain is separate but sticks to its neighbour. Cover with a damp cloth; never refrigerate it.
5. **Slice the fish.** For nigiri, cut across the grain at a 45° angle into 8g slices, roughly 5x2.5cm, in **one single pull of the knife** — no sawing, which shreds the flesh and dulls the surface.
6. **Form nigiri.** Wet your hands in *tezu* (water with a splash of vinegar). Take 15g of rice and form it into a light oblong with three fingers, applying only just enough pressure to hold. Smear a trace of wasabi on the fish, drape it over the rice, and press once with two fingers, then turn and press the sides. The rice should hold together to the mouth and fall apart in it.
7. **Maki.** Nori shiny-side down on the mat, a thin even layer of rice with a 2cm bare strip at the top, filling in a line, and roll away from you, tightening the mat as you go. Cut with a wet blade, wiping between cuts.

## What you're learning
Rice as the technical centrepiece, plus knife discipline where a single stroke shows on the
plate. Pressure control in forming nigiri is genuinely hard, and the failure — a dense
puck — is invisible until someone bites it.

## Push it further
Weigh ten nigiri; get every one within a gram. Then cure a piece of mackerel in salt and
vinegar (*shime saba*) and learn what curing does to texture and shelf life.
