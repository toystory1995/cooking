Two components cooked separately to the wrong doneness on purpose, then finished together
in sealed steam. Timing and judgement, with no way to check on it once it is closed.

## Ingredients
- 800g bone-in chicken or lamb shoulder, in large pieces
- **Marinade:** 300g yoghurt · 2 tbsp ginger-garlic paste · 1 tsp turmeric · 2 tsp chilli powder · 2 tsp garam masala · juice of 1 lemon · salt · handful chopped mint and coriander
- 500g basmati rice
- 4 onions, sliced thin, fried to *birista*
- Whole spices: 4 green cardamom, 2 black cardamom, 1 cinnamon, 4 cloves, 2 bay, 1 tsp cumin, 1 tsp shahi jeera
- Big pinch saffron in 4 tbsp warm milk
- 4 tbsp ghee, mint and coriander, 2 tbsp fried cashews and raisins

## Method
1. **Marinate** the meat for at least 4 hours, ideally overnight. The yoghurt's acid and enzymes tenderise, and it will also stop the meat seizing in the dry heat of the dum.
2. **Birista.** Fry the sliced onions in ghee slowly until deep brown and crisp — 25 minutes, and the last three decide it. Drain on paper. Half goes into the marinade, half on top. Burnt birista makes the whole pot bitter.
3. **Soak** the rice 30 minutes, then drain.
4. **Parboil.** Boil a large pot of water, salted like pasta water and heavily spiced with the whole spices. Add the rice and cook **only to 70%** — about 4 minutes, so the grain still has a hard white core when you crush it. It finishes in the steam; fully cooked rice here means mush later.
5. **Layer.** Marinated meat in the bottom of a heavy pot, then the rice, then birista, herbs, saffron milk, ghee, nuts. Do not stir. Ever.
6. **Seal.** Lid on with a rope of dough pressed around the rim, or a double layer of foil then the lid. Cook on high for 4 minutes to get the steam going, then on the lowest possible heat — or on a tawa/griddle underneath as a heat diffuser — for **35–40 minutes**. Do not open it.
7. Rest 10 minutes off the heat, then break the seal at the table. Dig down with a flat spoon and lift, so the layers stay visible on the plate.

## What you're learning
Cooking two things to different stages so they finish together — the fundamental skill in
any composed dish. Also cooking blind: once the pot is sealed, the outcome depends entirely
on judgements you made twenty minutes earlier.

## Push it further
Make a vegetable version, where there is no meat fat and no long-cooking protein, so the
whole thing must be built from spice and ghee. Then compare *kacchi* (raw marinated meat)
with *pakki* (pre-cooked meat) biryani.
