The most demanding thing in the library that is mostly waiting. Nothing about sourdough is
on a clock — it is all judged by look, smell and feel, which is why it teaches so much.

## Ingredients
**Starter (from scratch, 7 days):** 50g wholemeal flour + 50g water daily, discarding half each day

**Loaf:** 400g strong white flour · 100g wholemeal · 350g water (70%) · 100g active starter · 10g salt

## Method
1. **Starter.** Mix flour and water in a jar. Each day, discard all but 50g and feed it 50g flour and 50g water. By day 5–7 it should double in 4–6 hours and smell of yoghurt and apples, not acetone. Use it at its peak — domed and just beginning to flatten.
2. **Autolyse.** Mix the flours and water, rest 1 hour.
3. Add the starter, squeeze it through, rest 30 minutes. Add the salt with a splash of water and squeeze again.
4. **Bulk ferment**, 4–6 hours at 22°C, with a stretch-and-fold every 45 minutes for the first three hours. Judge it by rise, not by clock: it is ready at roughly **50% larger**, domed, jiggly, with bubbles at the edges of the tub. Under-fermented dough bakes dense and gummy; over-fermented dough spreads flat and tastes sour and sharp.
5. **Pre-shape** onto an unfloured bench, dragging the dough towards you against the surface to build a taut skin. Rest 20 minutes uncovered.
6. **Shape** into a tight boule or bâtard, sealing the seam. The tension you build here is what makes the loaf rise up rather than out.
7. Into a floured banneton, seam-side up. **Cold retard** in the fridge 12–16 hours — this develops flavour and makes scoring far easier.
8. **Bake.** Preheat the dutch oven at 250°C for 45 minutes. Turn the loaf out onto paper, score once, decisively, at a shallow angle across the top. Lid on, 20 minutes; lid off, drop to 220°C, 20–25 minutes to a deep dark crust. The lid traps steam, which keeps the surface elastic while the loaf springs.
9. **Cool at least 2 hours** before cutting. Cutting hot bread ruins the crumb — the starch is still setting.

## What you're learning
Reading a living process. Every judgement here — starter peak, bulk rise, shaping tension,
crust colour — is sensory, and the loaf gives you a full report every time you cut it.

## Push it further
Bake the same recipe three weeks running and photograph the crumb. Then push hydration to
80% and see how much harder the dough is to handle for how much more open a crumb.
