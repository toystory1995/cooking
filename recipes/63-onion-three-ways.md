The cheapest ingredient in the kitchen, treated as if it were expensive. This is a plating
exercise disguised as a dish, and it costs about two pounds to practise.

## Ingredients
- 6 large onions, 4 shallots, 1 bunch spring onions
- 500ml chicken or vegetable stock
- Butter, cream, sherry vinegar, thyme, salt
- Optional to finish: chive oil, onion ash, a soft-boiled egg or a piece of grilled fish

## Method
1. **Sweet purée.** Slice 3 onions and cook them very slowly in butter with a pinch of salt, covered, for 40 minutes without colour, then uncovered for 15 to concentrate. Blend with a little cream and stock and pass through a fine sieve. Season with a few drops of vinegar.
2. **Charred.** Halve 2 onions through the root, cut side down in a dry hot pan, and leave them until the cut face is almost black. Then add stock, thyme and butter to the pan, cover, and braise in a 180°C oven for 30 minutes. Separate the petals — each is a vessel.
3. **Raw and pickled.** Slice the shallots into rings and steep in warm sherry vinegar with a little sugar and salt for 30 minutes. Keep some spring onion green, sliced fine, raw.
4. **Crisp.** Slice one onion very thin, dust with cornflour, and fry at 160°C until golden. Drain, salt.
5. **Now plate.** Work on the biggest plates you have and follow four rules:
   - **Odd numbers and one focal point.** Three petals, not four. One place the eye lands.
   - **Height at the back or centre**, never spread flat across the whole plate.
   - **Leave the rim empty** — a clean 3cm border makes everything inside look deliberate.
   - **Sauce goes down first**, dragged or spooned, never poured over the top at the end.
6. Build: a wide sweep or dots of purée, charred petals leaning against each other, pickled rings tucked in, crisps on last so they stay crisp, a few drops of chive oil in the sauce.
7. Photograph it. Then wipe the plate, rebuild it differently, and photograph again. Compare.

## What you're learning
Composition. Height, contrast, odd numbers, negative space and the order of assembly — plus
the habit of building a plate twice, which is how you find out that your first instinct is
usually cluttered.

## Push it further
Repeat with a single protein and three vegetables. Then set yourself the hardest version:
one plate, three elements only, that still looks complete.
