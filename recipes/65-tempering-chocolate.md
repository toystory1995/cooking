Chocolate is the one ingredient with a right and a wrong crystal structure, and the
difference is visible, audible and completely unforgiving.

## Ingredients
- 400g good dark couverture, 60–70%, in callets or chopped fine
- That is the entire ingredients list. Everything else is temperature.

## Method
1. **Why.** Cocoa butter sets in six crystal forms. Only form V gives a glossy surface, a clean snap and a chocolate that releases from a mould and does not bloom. Tempering means melting the lot, then encouraging only form V to grow.
2. **Melt** two-thirds of the chocolate over a bain-marie — bowl not touching the water — to **50–55°C**. Never let a drop of water in: chocolate seizes instantly and cannot be brought back.
3. **Seeding method (easiest).** Off the heat, add the remaining third of unmelted chocolate and stir constantly. The cool solid chocolate carries the crystals you want and drags the temperature down. Cool to **28–29°C**.
4. **Reheat** very gently to the working temperature: **31–32°C** for dark, 30–31°C for milk, 29–30°C for white. A few seconds too long and you have melted your seed crystals and must start again.
5. **Tabling method (the classic).** Pour two-thirds of the melted chocolate onto a marble slab and work it back and forth with a palette knife and scraper until it thickens and reaches 28°C, then scrape it back into the remaining warm chocolate to bring the whole lot to 31–32°C.
6. **Test.** Dip the tip of a knife and leave it at cool room temperature for 3 minutes. Properly tempered chocolate sets matte-to-glossy, firm and unstreaked. Streaks, dullness or a soft finish mean out of temper — remelt and start again. Test every time; it costs three minutes and saves the batch.
7. Use it: mould bars, dip fruit, spread thin on acetate and cut shapes as they set for plating, or pipe filigree.
8. Keep it in the working window by resting the bowl on a warm cloth and stirring often.

## What you're learning
That some ingredients have a physical structure you must control rather than a flavour you
must season — and the habit of testing a small sample before committing. Tempering is
mostly a thermometer and the discipline to start over.

## Push it further
Make filled bonbons in a polycarbonate mould: shell, fill with a ganache made at 35°C, cap
and turn out. The moment a properly tempered shell drops out of its mould with a click is
worth the effort.
