Notorious, and not because they are hard to make — because they are hard to make
*repeatably*. Every fault has a specific cause, which is what makes them worth learning.

## Ingredients
- 150g ground almonds
- 150g icing sugar
- 110g egg whites, split into two lots of 55g, aged uncovered in the fridge for a day
- 150g caster sugar, 50ml water
- Gel colouring (never liquid)
- Filling: ganache, buttercream or curd

## Method
1. **Sift** the ground almonds and icing sugar together twice, discarding coarse bits. Any lump becomes a bumpy shell.
2. Mix 55g of the whites into the dry mixture to a stiff paste. Add colour here.
3. **Italian meringue.** Boil the caster sugar and water to **118°C**. When the syrup hits about 110°C start whisking the other 55g of whites to soft peaks. Pour the syrup in a thin steady stream down the side of the bowl, then whisk until the meringue is glossy, holds a bird's beak, and the bowl has cooled to about 40°C.
4. **Macaronage** — the step that decides everything. Fold a third of the meringue into the almond paste to slacken it, then the rest, then keep folding and pressing the batter against the side of the bowl to deflate it deliberately. Stop when the batter falls from the spatula in a **continuous ribbon that sinks back into itself in about 10 seconds**. Under-mixed gives peaked, cracked shells; over-mixed gives flat shells with no feet.
5. Pipe 3.5cm rounds, vertically, on a template under the paper. Rap the tray hard on the bench three times to release air bubbles, and pop any that surface with a cocktail stick.
6. **Rest** 30–60 minutes until the surface is dry to a fingertip. This skin is what forces the rise downwards into the frilled foot.
7. Bake at 150°C for 14–16 minutes. They are done when a shell does not wobble on its foot when nudged.
8. Cool completely on the tray. Fill, then **mature in the fridge for 24 hours** — the shell softens from the filling and the texture changes completely. Unmatured macarons taste like sweet biscuits.

## Fault-finding
- **Cracked tops:** insufficient resting, or the oven too hot.
- **No feet:** over-mixed batter, or too much moisture in the kitchen.
- **Hollow shells:** over-whipped meringue or too high a bake temperature.
- **Lopsided feet:** uneven oven, or piping at an angle.

## What you're learning
Diagnosis. Macarons fail loudly and specifically, so they train you to look at a result and
name its cause — the most valuable habit in a competition kitchen.

## Push it further
Make one batch with the French method (raw whites, no syrup) and compare consistency across
three attempts. Then flavour the shells with freeze-dried fruit powder rather than colour.
