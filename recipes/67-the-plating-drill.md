Not a new dish — the same dish, plated three ways, so that plating becomes a decision
rather than an afterthought.

## The brief
Cook one thing you already know well from this library. A piece of fish with a purée and a
sauce works perfectly. Make enough for three servings, then plate it three times:

1. **Classical.** Protein at the front, garnish behind, sauce poured around the base. Centred, symmetrical, generous. This is what most food should look like.
2. **Modern restaurant.** Off-centre, height at the back, sauce dragged or dotted, a wide empty rim, one textural element on top, odd numbers of everything.
3. **Rustic.** Family-style in a shallow bowl, components overlapping, herbs scattered, deliberately unfussy — which is much harder to make look intentional than it sounds.

## The rules
- **Sketch each one on paper first.** Thirty seconds each. Plating without a plan is how a plate ends up crowded.
- **Warm the plates** for hot food, chill them for cold. A cold plate loses you 10°C in the first minute.
- **Sauce goes down before the protein**, always, or you end up pouring it over something you have just made crisp.
- **The last thing you touch should be the smallest.** Herbs, crisps and powders go on after everything is placed.
- **Wipe the rim.** Every time, with a cloth and a little vinegar.
- Photograph each plate under the same light, from a 45° angle and from directly above.

## The debrief
Look at the three photographs side by side, an hour later, and answer:
1. Which plate makes you want to eat it? Which looks best in the photograph? They are often not the same one, and knowing the difference matters.
2. What could you remove from each without losing anything?
3. Where is the eye going first? Is that where you wanted it?
4. Did any element go cold while you fiddled? What would you place first next time?

## What you're learning
That plating is a set of repeatable decisions — height, focal point, negative space, order
of assembly, temperature — and not an artistic gift. Also self-editing: the most common
plating fault by a distance is too much on the plate.

## Push it further
Do the drill again with a brown, ugly dish — a stew, a curry, a braise. Making genuinely
delicious brown food look good is the real test, and it is what most home cooking is.
