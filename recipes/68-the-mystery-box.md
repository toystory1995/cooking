Repeatable, and worth repeating monthly. The skill being tested is not cooking — it is
deciding quickly and then committing.

## The setup
Have someone else choose **six to eight ingredients** and put them on the bench without
telling you in advance. Rules for whoever is choosing:

- One protein, two or three vegetables or fruit, one dairy or egg, one starch, one wildcard (a spice, a vinegar, an oily tin, something pickled).
- At least one thing you would not normally buy.
- You may use salt, pepper, oil, butter, flour, sugar and vinegar freely from your own store cupboard. Nothing else.

## The clock
**75 minutes**, one plate, cooked and plated. Set the timer where you can see it.

## How to use the first five minutes — and they matter more than the last twenty
1. **Taste or smell anything you are unsure of** before you plan.
2. **Choose your cooking method first**, driven by the clock. Under 75 minutes rules out braising a tough cut, and rules in searing, roasting, frying, poaching and raw.
3. **Write the plate down**: protein, starch or vegetable, sauce, texture, acid, herb. If you cannot name a sauce and an acid, you do not have a dish yet.
4. **Write a timeline backwards** from plating. Longest cook goes on first.
5. **Then commit.** Changing your mind at minute 40 is what loses these rounds, not a wrong idea at minute 5.

## Rules of thumb when you are stuck
- Anything roasted goes with anything acidic.
- Fat needs acid; sweetness needs salt; starch needs both.
- If you have no sauce, you have a purée, an emulsion, or pan juices with butter — one of those three is always available.
- One thing crisp, one thing soft, one thing sharp.
- Cook the protein last and rest it while you plate.

## The debrief
Photograph it, then write down: what you decided in the first five minutes, what you changed
and why, what you ran out of time for, and what the plate was missing. Keep these notes —
after six mystery boxes the same weaknesses show up in your own handwriting.

## What you're learning
Decision speed under constraint, which is the actual skill being tested in an invention
test. Everything else you have learned in this library is what you draw on; this is
practising the drawing.

## Push it further
Harder versions: five ingredients, no recipe you have cooked before, a dessert only, or 45
minutes instead of 75. Then the cruellest one — someone else plates your food.
