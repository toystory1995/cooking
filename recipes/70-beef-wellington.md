The showpiece. Four separate preparations that must reach the same moment together, and
one enemy throughout: water.

## Ingredients
- 800g centre-cut beef fillet, trimmed and tied
- 400g chestnut mushrooms
- 2 shallots, 2 garlic cloves, 1 sprig thyme
- 100ml Madeira or dry sherry
- 12 slices prosciutto
- 2 tbsp English mustard
- 500g all-butter puff pastry
- 6 thin crêpes (optional but the safest insurance)
- 2 egg yolks beaten with a pinch of salt

## Method
1. **Sear.** Season the fillet hard. Sear in a smoking-hot pan on every surface, 60 seconds a face, then the ends. Brush all over with mustard while hot and cool completely.
2. **Duxelles.** Blitz mushrooms, shallots and garlic to a fine crumb. Cook in a dry wide pan over medium-high heat for 20–25 minutes: it releases a surprising amount of water, then that water evaporates and the mixture darkens and tightens. Add Madeira and thyme and cook until **completely dry**. Cool. Wet duxelles is the number one cause of a soggy base.
3. **Wrap.** Lay overlapping cling film on the bench. Cover with overlapping prosciutto in a rectangle. Spread the duxelles over it in an even layer. Sit the fillet at one edge and roll it up tightly using the film, twisting the ends like a cracker to compact it into an even cylinder. Chill 30 minutes.
4. If using crêpes, lay them over fresh film, unwrap the beef onto them and roll again — a second waterproof layer between filling and pastry.
5. **Pastry.** Roll the puff to 4mm. Unwrap the beef onto it, brush the pastry border with egg yolk, and roll it over, sealing the seam underneath. Trim the ends, fold and tuck. Chill 30 minutes — cold pastry puffs, warm pastry slumps.
6. Egg wash, chill 15 minutes, wash again, then score a pattern with the back of a knife without cutting through. Sprinkle with flaky salt.
7. **Bake** at 200°C for 35–40 minutes to **52°C** at the centre for medium-rare. Probe through the end, not the top.
8. **Rest 15 minutes**, uncovered so the pastry stays crisp. It will carry over to about 56°C.
9. Carve with a very sharp knife in confident 3cm slices. Serve with a red wine jus made from the trimmings.

## What you're learning
Moisture management as a design problem — every layer here exists to keep water away from
pastry — and the discipline of chilling between stages, which is what stops a Wellington
falling apart.

## Push it further
Make individual Wellingtons: harder, because the surface-to-centre ratio changes and the
timings compress. Then attempt a salmon *en croûte* with the same logic.
