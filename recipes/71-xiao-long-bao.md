The hardest dumpling in the library, and a genuine benchmark: a liquid filling held in a
wrapper thin enough to see through, closed with eighteen pleats, that must not leak. The
soup is not poured in — it is set into jelly, wrapped cold, and melted by the steam.

## Ingredients
**Aspic:** 500g pork skin (or 2 trotters, split) · 1 chicken carcass · 2 litres water · ginger · 2 spring onions · 2 tbsp Shaoxing · salt

**Filling:** 400g pork mince, 30% fat · 2 tbsp ginger juice · 1 tbsp light soy · 1 tsp sugar · 1 tsp salt · white pepper · 1 tsp sesame oil

**Wrappers:** 300g plain flour · 150ml cold water · ½ tsp salt

**To serve:** black vinegar with fine ginger slivers

## Method
1. **Aspic, day one.** Blanch the skin and bones for 5 minutes, rinse, then simmer with the aromatics for 4 hours at a bare tremble. Strain, season lightly, and chill overnight. It must set **firm enough to dice** — if it wobbles like a panna cotta, reduce it further and re-set. This is collagen doing exactly what it did in the bourguignon, harnessed for texture instead of richness.
2. Dice the set aspic into 5mm cubes. Fold it through the seasoned pork mince — roughly equal weights of aspic and meat — and keep it in the fridge. The filling must stay cold and firm right up to the moment it is wrapped.
3. **Wrappers.** Knead flour, salt and cold water into a firm dough, 10 minutes. This is a cold-water dough, unlike the jiaozi: you want gluten strength, because the wrapper must be rolled very thin and still hold liquid. Rest 1 hour.
4. Roll 8–10g pieces into 7cm circles, thick centre and a rim you can almost see through. Work in batches of six and keep the rest covered.
5. **The pleat.** Hold the wrapper in a cupped hand, 15g of filling in the middle. Make a pleat at the rim, then another beside it, pushing each into the last and rotating — aim for eighteen, and never let the filling touch the rim. Close by twisting the collected pleats and pinching the top off cleanly. A leaky dumpling is nearly always a greasy rim, not a bad pleat.
6. Sit each on a small square of paper in the steamer with a finger's width between them.
7. Steam over a **rolling** boil for exactly **8 minutes**. Underdone and the aspic has not melted; overdone and the wrapper splits and the soup is gone.
8. Eat: lift by the topknot into a spoon, nip the side, drink the soup, then eat the rest with vinegar and ginger.

## What you're learning
Everything the dumpling track has taught, at once — plus the trick of changing an
ingredient's *state* to solve a problem. You cannot wrap soup, so you make it a solid, wrap
it, and let the steam turn it back. That kind of thinking is exactly what an invention test
rewards.

## Push it further
Make ten. Steam three, adjust, steam three more. Then serve six to someone else and ask
them how many leaked — an honest answer is worth more than your own count.
