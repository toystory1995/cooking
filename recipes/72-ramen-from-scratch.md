Four separate crafts that meet in one bowl, each of which is a discipline in itself. This is
the biggest single project in the library and it is best spread over two days.

## Ingredients
**Broth (chintan or paitan):** 2kg chicken carcasses and wings · 500g pork trotter (for a rich paitan) · 1 onion · 1 leek · garlic · ginger · dried shiitake · water to cover

**Tare (shio):** 100ml sake · 50ml mirin · 60g salt · 20g kombu · 15g katsuobushi · 200ml water

**Aroma oil:** 100ml neutral oil · sliced spring onion greens · garlic · ginger

**Noodles:** 300g strong flour · 130ml water · 4g kansui (or 4g baked baking soda) · 3g salt

**Toppings:** chashu pork belly rolled and braised in soy, mirin and sake · *ajitama* eggs boiled 6½ minutes then marinated overnight in the chashu liquid · menma, nori, spring onion

## Method
1. **Broth, day one.** Blanch the bones 10 minutes, rinse them scrupulously. For a **clear** broth, simmer at 85°C for 6 hours and never let it boil. For a **paitan**, boil it hard for 6–8 hours, topping up, until the fat and collagen emulsify into an opaque, creamy liquid. These are opposite techniques and give opposite results — choose deliberately.
2. **Tare.** Simmer the sake and mirin to burn off alcohol, add the water, salt and kombu, warm to 60°C, then off the heat add the katsuobushi and steep 20 minutes. Strain. This is the seasoning — the broth itself stays unsalted, which is why ramen shops can serve three bowls from one pot.
3. **Aroma oil.** Warm the oil with the aromatics at 120°C for 20 minutes, then strain.
4. **Chashu.** Roll and tie the belly, brown it, then braise in equal parts soy, sake, mirin and water with sugar at 140°C for 3 hours. Cool in the liquid, chill, then slice and blowtorch or sear to order. The eggs marinate in this same liquid, diluted.
5. **Noodles.** Dissolve the kansui and salt in the water — this alkalinity is what makes ramen noodles yellow, springy and slippery; without it you have pasta. Mix to a very dry, crumbly dough, then rest 30 minutes, and knead or roll repeatedly until smooth. Roll to 1.5mm, cut thin, dust with cornflour. Rest a day if you can.
6. **Assembly, and this is the exam.** Bowls scalded with boiling water. Tare in the bottom, 30ml. Aroma oil on top. Broth poured in hard from height so it emulsifies with the oil. Noodles boiled **90 seconds**, drained hard — shaken dry, because water dilutes the bowl — and folded into the broth. Toppings placed, never dropped. Nori last, at the edge, so it stays crisp.
7. From noodle to table: under 60 seconds.

## What you're learning
Component cooking at full scale, and separated seasoning — the idea that the broth, the
salt and the fat are three independent variables you can tune. Also assembly speed, which
is a skill nobody practises until they need it.

## Push it further
Make a second bowl with the same broth but a soy tare and a different oil, and taste how
much the base changes. Then do a mazemen — brothless — where the tare has nowhere to hide.
