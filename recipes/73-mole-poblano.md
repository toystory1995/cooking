Over twenty ingredients, each cooked separately before they ever meet. It is the most
complex sauce in this library and the best argument that cooking is composition.

## Ingredients
**Chillies:** 6 ancho · 4 pasilla · 4 mulato · 2 chipotle — all stemmed and deseeded

**Fried separately:** 100g sesame seeds · 60g almonds · 60g raisins · 50g pumpkin seeds · 2 tomatoes · 3 tomatillos · ½ onion · 4 cloves garlic · 1 stale corn tortilla · 1 slice stale bread · 1 stick cinnamon · 4 cloves · 6 peppercorns · 1 tsp coriander seed · 1 tsp anise

**Plus:** 45g dark chocolate (or Mexican tablet chocolate) · 1.5 litres chicken stock · lard or oil · salt · a little sugar

**To serve:** poached and shredded chicken or turkey, rice, sesame seeds

## Method
1. **Toast the chillies** in a dry pan, 20–30 seconds a side, pressing them flat, until they smell sweet and smoky and turn pliable. **The moment one blackens the whole mole is bitter** — this is the step that decides the dish. Soak them in hot stock for 30 minutes.
2. Now fry each remaining ingredient **separately** in lard, in order of how long it takes, removing each to a bowl as it colours: nuts and seeds until golden, bread and tortilla until crisp, raisins until they puff, aromatics until soft, whole spices for seconds only. It takes an hour, and it is the point: each thing needs its own moment.
3. Blend in batches with the soaking stock until very smooth, then **pass through a sieve**, pushing hard. What stays behind is skin and grit; what goes through is the mole.
4. Heat a film of lard in the casserole until almost smoking, and pour the purée in all at once — it will splutter violently. This is *frying the sauce*: it darkens, thickens and loses its raw edge. Stir for 10 minutes.
5. Add stock to a pourable consistency and simmer, partly covered, for **at least 2 hours**, stirring often so the base does not catch. It should go from brick red to deep brown-black.
6. Add the chocolate and a little sugar in the last 20 minutes. Now taste and balance: salt for flatness, sugar against bitterness, a splash of vinegar or more chipotle for lift. Adjust in small steps over several minutes — it keeps changing.
7. Serve over poached chicken with rice, sesame seeds and a warm tortilla. It is better the next day.

## What you're learning
That complexity is built from separate, controlled steps, not from throwing things in
together — and how to hold twenty flavours in balance without any one arriving first. It is
also the strongest possible answer to the idea that European cooking has a monopoly on
technique.

## Push it further
Make a mole verde, which is fresh, fast and green, and a simple mole coloradito. Cooking
three from the same family tells you far more than making the famous one once.
