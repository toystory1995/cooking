Sugar is the most dangerous thing in a domestic kitchen — caramel is over 160°C and it
sticks to skin — and the most impressive garnish you can put on a plate. Respect it, work
with a bowl of iced water beside you, and never taste it with your finger.

## Ingredients
- 500g caster sugar
- 150ml water
- 50g liquid glucose (stops crystallisation — worth buying)
- 200g hazelnuts or almonds, toasted
- A pinch of salt, and neutral oil for the equipment

## The stages, which are the whole lesson
Sugar boiled with water passes through fixed stages as water leaves. Memorise these:

- **112–116°C soft ball** — fudge, buttercream, Italian meringue
- **118–120°C firm ball** — caramels, nougat
- **121–130°C hard ball** — marshmallow
- **149–154°C hard crack** — brittle, spun sugar, pulled sugar, cages
- **160–180°C caramel** — pale gold through amber to mahogany, then bitter, then burnt

## Method
1. **Wet caramel.** Sugar, water and glucose in the pan. Stir **only until the sugar dissolves**, then stop touching it — a single stirred crystal seeds the whole pan and turns it grainy. Brush the sides down with a wet pastry brush. Boil undisturbed, and watch the thermometer rather than the clock.
2. **Praline.** Take it to a deep amber, around 175°C, pour it over the toasted nuts on an oiled tray and leave it to set hard. Snap it into shards for garnish, or blitz it: first to a powder, then keep going until the nut oil releases and it becomes a loose praline paste. That paste goes into buttercream, mousseline and ice cream.
3. **Spun sugar.** Take a batch to hard crack, 152°C, then plunge the base of the pan into cold water for **three seconds** to stop the cooking. Wait until the syrup thickens and falls from a fork in fine threads. Flick it back and forth over an oiled rolling pin or two broom handles — the threads set instantly into a golden nest. Use it within an hour; humidity kills it.
4. **A cage.** Oil the back of a ladle. Drizzle syrup over it in a criss-cross lattice from a spoon, let it set for a minute, and lift the cage off with your thumbs. Sit it over a quenelle or a fondant.
5. **Cleaning.** Fill the pan with water and boil it — the sugar dissolves off. Never scrape it.

## Safety, and it is not optional
Keep a bowl of iced water at your elbow. If sugar lands on skin, plunge it straight in and
hold it there. No children or crowded benches. Long sleeves, dry hands, no distractions.

## What you're learning
Temperature as a set of hard thresholds rather than a gradient, and crystallisation control
— which is the same problem as tempering chocolate approached from the other direction.

## Push it further
Make a croquembouche with the choux from recipe 48, glued with caramel and wrapped in spun
sugar. It is the classic showpiece and it is genuinely difficult.
