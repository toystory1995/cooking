A modern patisserie showpiece: four components, built upside down, frozen solid, and glazed
in one pour. It is the dessert most likely to appear in a final, and it must be started at
least a day ahead.

## Ingredients
**Insert (passion fruit cremeux):** 150g passion fruit purée · 2 yolks · 1 egg · 40g sugar · 3g gelatine · 70g butter

**Mousse (white chocolate and vanilla):** 200g white chocolate · 250ml double cream, semi-whipped · 100ml milk · 5g gelatine · 1 vanilla pod

**Base:** a thin almond sponge or a crisp feuilletine layer of 100g chocolate, 60g praline paste and 60g crushed wafer

**Mirror glaze:** 150g sugar · 150g glucose · 75ml water · 100g condensed milk · 12g gelatine · 150g white chocolate · colouring

## Method
1. **Insert, day one.** Cook the purée, eggs and sugar to 82°C like a curd, off the heat add the bloomed gelatine, cool to 40°C and blend in the butter. Pour into a small ring or mould **narrower than your main mould** and freeze solid.
2. **Base.** Mix the melted chocolate, praline and wafer, spread 4mm thick, and cut a disc slightly smaller than the mould. Chill.
3. **Mousse.** Warm the milk with vanilla, add bloomed gelatine, pour over the white chocolate and emulsify with a stick blender. Cool to 30°C — critical: warmer and it deflates the cream, cooler and it sets before you fold. Fold in the semi-whipped cream in two additions.
4. **Assemble upside down.** Half fill the mould with mousse, push it up the sides with a spoon, press the frozen insert into the centre, top with mousse, level, then press the base disc on last. Freeze **overnight, solid all the way through**.
5. **Glaze.** Boil sugar, glucose and water to 103°C. Off the heat add condensed milk, then bloomed gelatine, then pour over the white chocolate. Blend with a stick blender kept **below the surface** — air bubbles are the enemy and they will show as craters. Strain, colour, then cool to exactly **32–35°C**.
6. Turn the frozen entremet out onto a rack over a tray. Pour the glaze in one confident movement from the centre outwards. Do not go back over it. Let it drip 2 minutes, trim the skirt with a palette knife, and lift onto the board with a spatula.
7. Defrost in the fridge for **4–6 hours** before serving. Slice with a hot dry knife.

## What you're learning
Working in reverse and in stages: every element is made, set and frozen before the next
goes on, which is how professional pastry copes with complexity. Also gelatine ratios, and
the very narrow temperature window that makes a glaze mirror rather than streak.

## Push it further
Change one variable at a time — a different insert, a sprayed velvet finish instead of a
glaze. Then make individual domes, which are eight chances to get the glaze right instead
of one.
