The last practical test in this library. Two courses, ninety minutes, plated and served hot
to somebody who is actually waiting — which is a different thing entirely from cooking for
yourself.

## The brief
- **A main and a dessert**, or a starter and a main. Two plates each for two people, so four plates in total.
- The two courses must **share at most one ingredient**, so you cannot hide behind a single prep.
- At least one element on each plate must be a technique from levels 3–5: an emulsion, a
  pastry, a set cream, a cured or fermented element, a filled pasta or dumpling.
- Both courses plated to the standard of the plating drill.
- Dessert served no more than 20 minutes after the main is cleared.

## Before you start
Write the timeline. It is not optional and it is the thing being tested. Work backwards
from service:

- **T-90 to T-70:** all prep, everything cut and weighed, bench cleared. Ovens on.
- **T-70 to T-40:** anything long — the braise, the pastry blind bake, the ice cream churning, the thing that must chill.
- **T-40 to T-15:** dessert assembled to the last step and set aside; sauces made and held warm.
- **T-15 to T-0:** protein cooked and rested, plates warmed, garnishes ready in a line.
- **T-0:** main plated in under four minutes.
- **Then** the last dessert step only — the quenelle, the glaze, the torch, the tuile.

Pin it up where you can see it. Cross each line off.

## The rules
- Clean as you go. If the bench is chaos at T-30 you have already lost the dessert.
- Taste everything twice: once when you make it, once before it goes on the plate.
- If something fails, decide within 60 seconds whether to fix it or drop it. A confident
  three-element plate beats a broken four-element one.
- No tasting spoon goes back in the pot.

## The debrief
Ask the person you served, honestly: was anything cold, was anything under-seasoned, and
which plate would they order again? Then write down where your timeline broke, because it
will have broken somewhere, and that place is your actual weakness.

## What you're learning
Everything at once, against a clock, for someone else — which is the only real simulation of
a competition kitchen you can run at home. Do this six times and the nerves stop being the
problem.

## Push it further
Three courses in two hours. Then the version that teaches most: cook the same two-course
menu twice in a week and see how much the second run improves purely from knowing the
timeline.
